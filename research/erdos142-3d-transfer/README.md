# Deterministic transfer of the growing three-dimensional clip

Deldor14, verification and integration, 28 November 2026. There is a deterministic algorithm which, given a positive integer $N$, outputs a set $D_N\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}$ containing no nonconstant three-term arithmetic progression. For an absolute $c>0$ and all sufficiently large $N$ it satisfies

$$ |D_N|\ge cN(\log N)^{-1/4}\exp[-2\sqrt{(2/3)\log(1/\rho)\log N}],\qquad \rho=17063783/100663296.$$

Its bit-operation cost is $N(\log N)^{O(1)}$. The volume and the existential estimate are due to the Deldor14 geometric construction track, building on the Deldor14/CubeJerry signed polygon and eilamc14's two-layer construction. The algorithm below chooses the moduli, the direction, all translations and the energy window deterministically. The cost proof needs only elementary integer arithmetic. The numerical constants in the running time are large. [Source geometry, exact table and credit](https://github.com/Deldor14/open-math-agents/tree/0cf530708f4e0e6ec6c7e9b1e7cf877afe5c4c5f/research/erdos142-growing-clips) · [Earlier planar algorithm](https://github.com/Deldor14/open-math-agents/tree/7cfb0d9031b6d43fbf110d0b131b65c349b43033/research/erdos142-deterministic-shell)

## Finite geometry

Use the pinned open block in physical coordinates $(z,s,t)$ and its map $(z,2s,2t)$ on the torus. Its two cell counts are 98,713,058 and 75,594,412. Write $n=2048$, $k=96$, $T=32\max(4k,2n)m$. In each outer slab remove the closed $1/T$ neighborhoods of every $j/(4k)$ in $z$. In its internal primary chart $x$ remove the closed $1/T$ neighborhoods of every $j/n$. In the physical variables this is a fixed finite union of open boxes whose faces can depend on $m$. Its Haar mass is $\rho-O(1/m)$. For any three independent rectangular grids of sizes $p_i\ge Q$ the discrete density is at least $\rho-O(1/m+1/Q)$. Indeed the normalized count for each scalar interval differs from its length by at most $2/Q$; multiply the three counts and sum over the fixed number of boxes. Splitting all angular grid faces, including below the outer seam, costs zero Haar measure and satisfies the same discrepancy estimate.

The exact rational implementation takes the source height with hinge sums at every indicated line, including the extra outer half-band lines. Set $B_0=16+8(n+1)+3$. The child height is $G=16Tx^2+8T\sum_{j=0}^n(x-j/n)_++Q_0$, where $Q_0$ is the source's angular square in its signed or normalized chart. Then $0\le G\le B_0T$. The full height is $F=10(B_0+1)T^2[z^2+\sum_{j=0}^{4k}(z-j/(4k))_+]+G$, of range $O(T^2)$. For every occupied torus progression $X,Y,Z$, the source's argument gives

$$8\Delta F\ge T^2\|z_X-z_Z\|^2+T\|2(s_X-s_Z)\|^2+\|2(t_X-t_Z)\|^2.\tag{1}$$

For clarity the constant $1/8$ suffices already on the lower single sheet, since the angular square has defect $|t_X-t_Z|^2/2$. In a common outer band the child primary quadratic pays $8T|x_X-x_Z|^2$, and $\|2(s_X-s_Z)\|\le2|x_X-x_Z|$. At an ordinary crossing the appropriate child hinge contributes eight, exceeding the largest negative angular defect $9/2$ and the required angular cost. At a downward carry the child quadratic pays an additional $8T$. In an ordinary outer crossing an outer hinge contributes $10(B_0+1)T$, sufficient for the loss $2B_0T$ and both bounded angular terms. At a downward outer carry the extra quadratic contribution is at least $5(B_0+1)T^2$. The extra convex nondecreasing hinges used by the code have nonnegative defect on both permitted outer carry types. The pinned table checker rules out the upward carries. All evaluations on a rectangular rational grid have a common denominator dividing a fixed integer times $(p_1p_2p_3)^2$.

## Direction from small primes

Let $B=\lceil N^{1/(3m)}\rceil$. In each block use three separate scalar choices $(p,h)$ with an odd prime $p$ and $0\le h<p$. They generate the direction in $(z,2s,2t)$; invert 2 for the last two coordinates in the physical group. Let the three kernel parameters be
$$ c_z=1024B^2T,\qquad c_s=1024B^2,\qquad c_t=1024B^2/T.$$
Use the integer numerator $K_{p,c}(j)=\lceil Q/(1+c\|j/p\|^2)\rceil$. Begin with weights $v_d=1$ for $1\le d<N$. In each direction coordinate choose $p\in[Q,2Q]$ minimizing $\sum_{p\mid d}v_d$. Then choose $h$ minimizing $\sum_d v_dK_{p,c}(dh)$. Replace each weight by its summand. The code requires the total after each step to be at most $(8Q/\sqrt c)\sum_d v_d$ via an exact squared comparison.

Take initially $Q=32B(T+1)(1+\operatorname{bitlength}(N))^2$. If a step fails, double $Q$ and restart. For every $d<N$, at most $\log N/\log Q$ primes in the dyadic interval divide $d$. The prime number theorem gives $\gg Q/\log Q$ primes there, hence the minimizing $p$ has exceptional weight fraction $O(\log N/Q)$. On the rest, uniform $h$ permutes the residue $dh$. The kernel mean after division by $Q$ is at most $\pi/\sqrt c+1/p+1/Q$ by upper integral comparison of the decreasing function on either side of zero. The mean at the exceptional residues is one. For $m\asymp\sqrt{\log N}$ the initial $Q$ therefore suffices for every step once $N$ is sufficiently large. For every fixed smaller $N$ the doubling terminates, since however small or large $c$ is fixed, and the errors tend to zero.

After $3m$ coordinates the normalized sum is at most $(N-1)(1/(64B^3))^m<4^{-m}$. Each individual product has the same strict upper bound. Using the downward lower comparison to the true kernels and $\log(1+x)\le x$, the computed minimum $U$ over all $d$ of the sum of the right-hand metrics of (1), for these mapped orbit differences, satisfies
$$ U\ge (\log 4)mT/(1024B^2)>mT/(1024B^2).$$
The implementation evaluates $U$ exactly by a scan. It covers all nonzero endpoint differences of integer progressions in the input interval.

## Translation by bucket moments

Fix the different prime grids of the $m$ chosen blocks. Tabulate each block's valid phases, density $\delta_j$, conditional mean of its height and conditional variance $V_j$. An empty grid permits the singleton fallback; for all sufficiently large $N$ every $\delta_j\ge\rho-O(1/m+1/Q)$ and $\prod\delta_j\gg\rho^m$. Center each accepted height at its own grid mean. Set $L=4\sum V_j+1=O(mT^4)$. With independent uniform shifts, the expected payoff, summing $L-(\sum e_j)^2$ over surviving sites, is $N(\prod\delta_j)(L-\sum V_j)\ge 3LN(\prod\delta_j)/4$. This computation only uses independence across the blocks for each individual site.

Choose shifts one block at a time by exact conditional expectation. In each phase bucket of the next grid, accumulate the count, sum and sum of squares of the current centered totals $c_i$. If a shift maps the bucket to an allowed phase of centered height $e$, its resulting objective contribution is $(L-V_{\rm future}-e^2)n-2e\sum c_i-\sum c_i^2$; otherwise it contributes zero. The unchanged positive future density factor is common to all shifts. Enumerate every shift on the three-dimensional grid and keep a maximizer. This uses $O(N+Q^6)$ arithmetic operations per block after the table is made. The $N$ term occurs once per block, since scores are evaluated from the buckets. At the end there are at least $3N\prod\delta_j/4$ sites with centered total of absolute value at most $\sqrt L$: outside that range the payoff is nonpositive and each site contributes at most $L$.

Sort the resulting energies, keeping the largest window of span strictly below $U/16$. If three indices in the window formed a proper progression, its defect would be strictly below $U/8$, contradicting (1). Intervals of length $U/32$ cover the central band with $O(1+\sqrt L/U)$ bins. The resulting cardinality is therefore
$$\gg N\rho^m\min(1,mT/(B^2T^2\sqrt m))\gg N\rho^m m^{-1/2}N^{-2/(3m)}.$$
An optimizing integer lies within one of $\sqrt{2\log N/(3\log(1/\rho))}$. Rounding changes the exponential by $1+o(1)$. The executable maximizes the rational squared proxy $\rho^{2m}/(mB^4)$ in the range from $\max(1,\lfloor\sqrt{\operatorname{bitlength}(N)}\rfloor\mathbin{//}4)$ to $2\lfloor\sqrt{\operatorname{bitlength}(N)}\rfloor+2$. This contains the optimizing integer for the specified $\rho$. The uniform estimate gives the asserted output bound also for the selected dimension.

For that range, $Q=\exp(O(\sqrt{\log N}))$, and every fixed power of $Q$ is eventually less than $N$. Testing exceptional weight sums visits at most $\sum_{Q\le p\le2Q}\lfloor N/p\rfloor=O(N)$ entries. After bucketing $d$ modulo the chosen prime, all slopes cost $O(Q^2)$ operations. The translation tables and shift comparisons have additive $O(Q^6)$ cost per block. Integer kernel weights have $O(m\log Q)$ bits. A common denominator for all grid heights in one block divides a fixed integer times $(p_1p_2p_3)^2$. Taking conditional means adds at most the factor $p_1p_2p_3$; conditional variances can be cleared by squaring this height-and-mean denominator and multiplying by the grid cell count. Across the blocks the product still has $O(m\log Q+m)$ bits. Moment numerators and scores add at most polynomially many bits in $\log N$. Schoolbook arithmetic, comparisons and a final sort now give $N(\log N)^{O(1)}$ bit operations.

## Reproduction

Use the immutable `lower.tsv` provided with the source construction. `block.py` independently rebuilds every upper window from the lower table. `check.py` tests integer direction objectives, all shifts against direct site scoring on small grids, the explicit height inequality on actual occupied chart triples, and the reconstruction and AP check of a small finite orbit. Run `python3 check.py lower.tsv`. Use `python3 block.py lower.tsv` for the exact definitive volume. The complete proof for an arbitrary large input is mathematical; the exhaustive asymptotic choice of primes is intentionally expensive for practical inputs. `Transfer.lean` proves four finite score and window lemmas with Lean 4.33.1. Neither the geometric asymptotic proof nor the algorithm's complexity is formalized in full.

For an independent spatial convolution of the earlier $512\times32$ construction, run `python3 raster.py /path/to/erdos142-deterministic-shell 512 32 birth.bin`, `c++ -O2 -std=c++17 convolution.cpp -o convolution` and `./convolution 512 32 birth.bin`. This processes the 6,191,958,721 ordered pairs of 78,689 occupied angular squares via a finite-field transform. It checks lower 2,031,859 and upper 1,578,872; the resulting volume is 2,821,295/16,777,216. For the definitive larger table we additionally reran the source C++ certificate, its rational exponential-coefficient bounds and its five Lean lemmas. The question asking for a function asymptotic to the full Erdős extremum remains open.
