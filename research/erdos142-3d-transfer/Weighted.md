# Deterministic extraction at the positive quarter power

For the table of volume $\rho=17063783/100663296$, write $a=\log(1/\rho)$ and $C_3=2\sqrt{2a/3}$. A deterministic $N^{1+o(1)}$-bit-operation algorithm outputs an integer 3-AP-free set $D_N\subseteq[1,N]$ such that

$$\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{|D_N|}{N(\log N)^{1/4}\exp(-C_3\sqrt{\log N})}>2.18\cdot10^{-10}.$$

The nested energy, its middle-point ellipsoid and its explicit coefficient are due to the geometric construction track. We transfer its continuous experiment to a finite deterministic extractor, retaining the explicit numerical comparison. The [uniform-metric algorithm](README.md) attains the negative quarter power in $N(\log N)^{O(1)}$ bit operations. The full asymptotic equivalent remains open. ## Anchors and local expectations

Take $m$ within one of $\sqrt{2\log N/(3a)}$, let $M=2^{10m}$, and snap each physical scalar coordinate to the left end of its half-open $1/M$ bin. Use the exact charts and the two-level height $F=T(z,T(x,Q/3))$ from the source, with $T(u,g)=u^2+w(u)^2((2-g)^{-1}-1)/5$. Let $w_z,w_x$ be the distances to the ends of the current natural outer cell and internal primary cell. Use outer subdivisions $2k$ in the lower block and $4k$ in the upper. All blocks in this proof refer to the same fixed 2048 by 96 table.

Retain geometric anchors for which $w_z,w_x>\max(M^{-1/4},8/M)$ and the physical angle is more than $8/M$ from each $j/(2n)$, $n=2048$. The latter partition includes all original angular boundaries and signed seams. In the internal primary chart a physical shift of size $2/M$ has size at most $4/M$. Thus all physical points within $2/M$ of a retained anchor stay in its occupied piece. Full upper doubling preimages are included. Conditional on the chart, $F$ is uniformly Lipschitz, including across tent peaks: $w$ is 1-Lipschitz, the chart maps have slope at most two, and both perspective denominators lie in $[1,2]$. Write $s=\log(w_z^2w_x)$. The recorded signed score is negative, and selection requires its sum to be sufficiently large. Use these three finite labels:

$$E=\lfloor MF\rfloor,\quad Q_s=\lfloor m^2\log_2(w_z^2w_x)\rfloor,\quad B=\mathbf1_{60+1/4<2kz<60+3/4}.$$

Their product-coordinate composition is a pair of sums and a Boolean OR. The floor of the logarithm is obtained by a binary length comparison on an exact rational power. As in the source, the fixed marked interval has positive conditional mass, and its half-translate is separated from the occupied outer support.

Under Haar sampling followed by snapping, the retained one-block mass is $\delta_m=\rho-O(M^{-1/4})$, since every face belongs to a fixed finite box arrangement. Thus $\delta_m^m=(1-o(1))\rho^m$. On each cell the eroded domain has face discrepancy $O(1/M)$, $|s|=O(\log M)$ and $s$ is Lipschitz with constant $O(M^{1/4})$ within the trimmed cell. Its first two moments on the discrete grid converge to their continuous values. More quantitatively, discarding each collar $b=M^{-1/4}$ costs $O(b(1+|\log b|^2))$ in the first two moments. To see this, within each occupied box $z$ and $x$ range independently over their own intervals, with constant cross-section; integrate $|\log u|^j$ on $(0,b)$ for $j\le 2$. The tiny angular cuts are independent of $z$ and $x$ there. The Riemann error on the retained cells is $O(M^{-3/4}(1+\log M)^2)$. Hence the conditional mean of $-Q_s\log(2)/m^2$ equals the source mean $L$ plus $O(m^{-2}+M^{-1/4}(1+\log M)^2)$, and its variance is bounded independently of $m$. The bounded single-block grid height $E/M$ has variance tending to the source's $\sigma^2>0$; this follows from the same erosion bound and uniform Lipschitz estimate.

Put $v=m^{-1/4}$. Require the average decoded log loss to be at most $L+v$ and at least one marked coordinate. The exceptional conditional probability is $O(m^{-1/2})$ plus an exponentially small term. For any fixed $u>0$ the centered sum of accepted-grid heights lies within $u$ conditional standard deviations with probability tending to $\Pr(|Z|\le u)$ for $Z$ standard normal. For example the triangular-array Berry–Esseen inequality applies because the one-block third moments are uniformly bounded and the variances stay away from zero.

Use the source's $b_*$ and a width
$$W=(1-4v)\exp(-4v/3)b_*mN^{-2/(3m)}$$
for sufficiently large $m$. This has ratio tending to one to the source width, and $\log W=-am+O(\log m)$. In particular $m^j/M=o(W)$ for every fixed $j$. Partition the central band of total $E/M$ into consecutive bins of width $\lfloor MW\rfloor/M$, extending the last if necessary. After the two cuts some window has unconditional Haar-anchor probability
$$p_1\ge (1-o(1))\rho^m W\frac{\Pr(|Z|\le u)}{2u\sigma\sqrt m}.$$
Moving the two integer energy bounds to the first and last eligible attainable energies and moving the score threshold to its first eligible attained value leaves the eligible sites unchanged. The implementation searches all such parameter triples. It does not evaluate $L$, $\sigma$, $b_*$ or $W$.

## Conditional on the middle

In a genuine Haar affine orbit, conditional on the exact middle of a specified proper progression, its step is Haar. Fix that middle and let $y_0$ be its anchor. For any step $d$ giving three eligible anchors consider the true progression $y_0-d,y_0,y_0+d$. Its endpoints are within $2/M$ of their respective eligible anchors, including at a physical wrap. Thus it stays in the block and its three true sum heights lie in an interval of width $W'=W+O(m/M)$ at a common position independent of the step. In particular $W'/W=1+o(1/m)$. The middle is the exact eligible anchor; its true mean log loss is at most $L+v$ since rounding the signed score down can only increase the decoded loss. The derivative and unique inverse branch at a marked middle apply without perturbing that coordinate.

The source's bound, from its inequality (2) and the marked endpoint slice, bounds the conditional step mass by
$$O(\sqrt{mW'})\left(\frac{4\pi\mathrm e W'}{3m}\right)^{3m/2}(4\cdot320\cdot9600)^{m/2}\exp[m(L+v)].$$
With our $W$ this is $o(1/N)$. Indeed the extra factor $\sqrt{W'}$ cancels $\rho^{-m/2}$ up to a polynomial and $(1-4v)^{3m/2}$ dominates that polynomial. The conditioning is on the middle, so the three-site probability for a proper progression satisfies $p_3\le p_1 o(1/N)$, retaining the entire one-site mass. Counting ordered progressions changes only a constant here.

## Exact finite selection

Use the finite estimator of the linked planar paper, taking its product over **three** independent scalar maps per physical block. For a scalar choose $(p,h,b)$ from all triples with $M^2\le p\le2M^2$ prime and $0\le h,b<p$; send index $i$ to $((hi+b)\bmod p)/p$. Its reference single-cell law is uniform. Its reference triple law assigns weights $1,2,1$ to the third-cell offsets $-1,0,1$ from $2j-i$ when the first two cells are $i,j$ (normalize by $4M^2$). This follows by integrating squared-cell fractional coordinates. For a fixed proper index gap, the total mass of $p$ dividing the gap is $O(\log N/M^2)$. Good-prime atom error is $O(1/(Mp)+1/p^2)$ by the boundary-grid count for the three polygons. Bad primes contribute only diagonal cells, of conditional mass $O(1/M)$ each. The scalar relative errors are $O((1+\log N)/M)$ for upper triple comparison and $O(1/M)$ for lower single comparison. Multiplying three factors still gives respective bounds $1+\eta$ and $1-\eta$, $\eta=(m+1)^{-2}$, for the optimizing dimension and all sufficiently large $N$. Coarsening the cell labels and discarding bad sites preserves these one-sided bounds.

With $r$ unassigned blocks and any fixed prefix, let $S_r$ and $C_r$ be total reference continuation probabilities over sites and ordered nonconstant three-term progressions respectively. Average $(1-\eta)^{r-1}S_{r-1}-(1+\eta)^{r-1}C_{r-1}$ over the finite choices for the next block. The preceding comparisons and nonnegativity bound this average below by $(1-\eta)^r S_r-(1+\eta)^r C_r$. Select a maximizing block. At the initial step maximize the same score over all window/threshold choices, so the preceding good Haar window supplies a lower comparison $(1-o(1))Np_1$. At the final step delete each occupied middle of a proper progression; its number is bounded by the count of such progressions. The resulting set attains this comparison. After integer dimension rounding and then letting $u\downarrow0$, the source's lower comparison transfers unchanged:
$$\frac{b_*}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}\left(\frac2{3a}\right)^{1/4}>2.18213\cdot10^{-10}.$$
The actual variance comparison and the rounded numerical bound were rerun from the pinned geometric proof.

A physical Haar block uses at most $(3M^2)^3$ scalar-cell triples across the three dimensions and mass denominator $(4M^2)^3$. There are $N^{o(1)}$ energy/score/mark states for $m$ blocks and arity at most three; $|E|=O(M)$ per coordinate and $|Q_s|=O(m^3)$. The prime alphabet, all candidate blocks, all continuation-state convolutions and window loops have $N^{o(1)}$ elements. Exact weights have $O(m^2)$ bits. Weighted colour counts at a prefix use integer polynomial convolutions of endpoint indicators, reading coefficients at doubled middle indices and subtracting diagonals. The packed, growing-radix Toom implementation from the planar extractor gives $N^{1+o(1)}$ bit cost, including deletion; it is reproduced in `finite.py`. Try every $m\le2\lfloor\sqrt{\operatorname{bitlength}(N)}\rfloor+2$ and keep the largest cleaned output or the singleton. All such $M$ are $N^{o(1)}$. The implementation skips empty geometries when the required collar already covers every internal cell.

## Checks and scope

`python3 check_weighted.py lower.tsv` independently reconstructs all 4,096 scalar-product microcells of a small cubic Haar law. On a finite prime-127 alphabet with all possible phase patterns on $[1,5]$, direct atom comparisons validate the cubed constants and the estimator increases from $5/8$ to $4$, producing $\{1,2,4,5\}$. Independently recomputed decimal labels at 85-digit precision reproduce the exact rational model on 262 occupied anchors across the three chart types, with two marked anchors. The source's 14,640 rational midpoint tests and five Lean lemmas were rerun. The finite comparison lemmas are also supplied from the planar proof. The full asymptotic transfer is a written argument for review.
