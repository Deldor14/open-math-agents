# Eighteen primitive generalized Fermat signatures with input exponent 13

Deldor14 · 28 November 2026 · Written proof for independent mathematical review

**Theorem claimed.** Let
$$\mathcal P=\{71,89,113,137,167,179,191,227,251,269,383,503,509,641,643,661,743,947\}.$$
If $p\in\mathcal P$ and $a,b,c\in\mathbb Z$ satisfy $a^{13}+b^{13}=c^p$ and $\gcd(a,b,c)=1$, then $abc=0$. The claim is unconditional, with arbitrary signs and heights. We give per-prime certificates. The first sieve below is independent of the finite prime range in Best–Dahmen–Freitas §4.2. We use their modular reduction, the split descent of the coordinated degree-seven work and the one- and two-place division results cited below.

## The projective unit sieve

Suppose $abc\ne0$ and put $D=a+b$, $z=\zeta_{13}$, $L=\mathbb Q(z)$, $H=\mathbb Q(z+z^{-1})$, $M=H(\zeta_p)$. Theorem 1.2 of Best–Dahmen–Freitas gives $13\nmid D$. Their elementary cyclotomic descent (4.1)–(4.3), valid for the present prime exponents, gives $D=v^p$ and $a+bz=\epsilon\beta^p$ for a unit $\epsilon$ of $L$. The class number of $L$ is one. Also $N_{L/\mathbb Q}(a+bz)=c^p/D$ is a rational $p$th power. The Frey representation for a nontrivial solution is congruent to that of $E_{1,0}$ or $E_{1,1}$ by their Proposition 2.2 and the incompatibility at 13 with $E_{1,-1}$ in §4.3. These inputs hold for all $p\ge17$ here. [Best–Dahmen–Freitas, §§2,4](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.12092v1#S4)

The unit $\epsilon$ is real up to a root of unity: $\epsilon/\bar\epsilon$ is a root of unity by Kronecker and is 1 modulo $1-z$, so is a power of $z$. Multiplying by a suitable power of $z$ makes $\epsilon$ real. All roots of unity in $L$ are $p$th powers for these $p$. We use the following detection consequence of $\operatorname{Cl}(M)[p]=0$, verified separately in every row. If a real unit is a local $p$th power at all places of $L$ over $p$, it is already a $p$th power in $H$. First descend to $H$ locally by norm since $[L:H]=2$. After adjoining $\zeta_p$, the Kummer extension generated by a $p$th root is trivial at $p$, unramified away from $p$ by the unit condition, and totally imaginary. The Hilbert class field theorem makes it trivial. Descend back to $H$ by the norm since $[M:H]=p-1$. The same statement holds for units of $L$ modulo roots of unity.

Suppose first that $p\nmid D$. Use $t=a/D$ and $A=t+(1-t)z$, an element that is a unit at every prime above $p$ since the order of $p$ modulo 13 is greater than one. Its norm $t^{13}+(1-t)^{13}$ is a rational $p$th power, and $A/\bar A$ is a $p$th power by the preceding cyclotomic descent and the unit ratio. Work in the unramified cyclotomic algebra modulo $p^2$, with Frobenius $\sigma(z)=z^p$. If an integral element $B$ is a $p$th power then $B^p\equiv\sigma(B)\pmod{p^2}$: write $B=C^p$ and use $C^p\equiv\sigma(C)\pmod p$. Therefore
$$ A^p\sigma(\bar A)-\bar A^p\sigma(A)=0\pmod{p^2}.$$
This is linear in the second digit of $t$. Write $t=t_0+pk$, $0\le t_0<p$. The two $p$th powers are fixed by $t_0$ modulo $p^2$ and the two Frobenius terms are affine in $k$. In every row the vector coefficient of $k$ after dividing by $p$ is nonzero for every $t_0$. The verifier solves using one nonzero coefficient, tests the entire vector, then tests the rational norm to the power $p-1$ modulo $p^2$. Exactly three residues pass the first test and exactly $t=0,1$ pass both tests. Hence $p^2\mid ab$ in this chart. At $t=0,1$ the local value of $A$ is congruent modulo $p^2$ to $z$ or 1, each a $p$th power. Every element of $1+p^2\mathcal O$ in an unramified $p$-adic field is a $p$th power by the logarithm. Since $D=v^p$, detection now makes $\epsilon$ a global $p$th power.

If $p\mid D$ then $p^2\mid D$ and $a+bz\equiv a(1-z)\pmod{p^2}$. Its norm is congruent to $13a^{12}$ and is a $p$th power. Choose $12k\equiv-1\pmod p$. Then $a/13^k$ is a local $p$th power in $\mathbb Q_p$: its twelfth power is congruent to a $p$th power modulo $p^2$, and 12 is invertible modulo $p$. Thus, for the global unit
$$\mu=(13/(1-z)^{12})^k,$$
the ratio $(a+bz)/\mu$ is a local $p$th power at all places over $p$, since $12k+1$ is divisible by $p$. Detection makes $\epsilon/\mu$ a global $p$th power. This proves that every solution lies in the pure finite chart or this single extraneous class.

## The auxiliary prime for each exponent

For each $(p,q)$ in the table, $q\ne p,13$ is prime, $f=\operatorname{ord}_{13}(q)>1$ and $p\mid q^f-1$. In the extraneous class, if $q\nmid D$, the normalized $t=a/D\bmod q$ must pass
$$ \big((t+(1-t)z)/\mu\big)^{(q^f-1)/p}=1\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_q[z]/\Phi_{13}.$$
All components are units and $D$ is a $p$th power. Enumerating **all** $q$ residues gives the empty set in every row, so $q\mid D$. The reduction of $E_{a,b}$ at $q$ would then be that of $E_{1,-1}$ by homogeneity and $q\nmid a$. All three model curves have good reduction by $q\not\equiv1\pmod{13}$ and the discriminant formula. We compare traces at a place of $L$ above $q$, after restricting the residual representations. The triple in the table gives these traces modulo $p$ in the order $E_{1,-1},E_{1,0},E_{1,1}$. In every row its first component differs from both others. This eliminates the extraneous class, including the chart $p\mid D$. [Billerey–Chen–Dieulefait–Freitas, §7.1, pp. 8665–8666](https://lmbp.uca.fr/~billerey/Research/Published/BCDF19_MultiFrey.pdf)

To verify these counts independently, write $h=1+\sum_{j\in\{1,3,4\}}(z^j+z^{-j})$, $h^2=h+3$. The three short Weierstrass models have $(a_4,a_6)$ respectively
```
(-2106+702h, 42120-16497h), (-189+27h, 837-270h), (-270-162h, -1188-783h).
```
They are obtained by substituting $(1,-1),(1,0),(1,1)$ into the cited formulas. The C verifier counts the points in the quadratic residue field, or in the prime field at a split place. It computes the quadratic character via the norm when the degree is two. The trace over the degree-$f$ extension follows by the Frobenius recurrence $T_n=T_1T_{n-1}-Q T_{n-2}$, with $T_0=2$ and $Q$ the size of the original residue field. It matches every tabulated residue independently of PARI elliptic routines.

## The global descent criterion

Write $S=ab/D^2$; the finite chart gives $v_p(S)\ge2$. Given two conjugates $d_i=2-z^i-z^{-i}$ and $d_j$, put $h'=d_j/d_i$, $U=h'(h'-1)$, $Y_0=2h'-1$, $W=2d_j-d_i$. The two conjugates of $\beta\bar\beta/v^2$ for the pure power satisfy $G_i^p=1-d_iS$ and $G_j^p=1-d_jS$. Thus
$$X=G_i/G_j^2,\qquad Y=(Y_0-d_jS)/(1-d_jS)$$
gives a point near $(1,Y_0)$ on $Y^2=1+4UX^p$. Use $i=1,j=2$ except at $p=71$, where the standard pair has zero trace. For the standard pair in the variable $x=z+z^{-1}$ the three quantities are $U=(x+1)(x+2)$, $Y_0=2x+3$, $W=6+x-2x^2$. For 71 use $i=1,j=6$ and $x=z^6+z^{-6}$, so that $d_i=4-x^2$, $d_j=2-x$, $U=-(x+1)/(x+2)^2$, $Y_0=-x/(x+2)$ and $W=x(x-2)$. The required units $U,Y_0$ in each model follow from their norms with the sextic minimal polynomial. The alternative pair and its nonvanishing at inert primes were independently described in the coordinated degree-eleven work. [Alternative conjugates and exact traces](https://github.com/Deldor14/open-math-agents/tree/c3b9c003925da482c69d38aaba51dafc5bebb8d0/research/beal-thirteenth-gap/inert-traces)

Here are the hypotheses and argument of the division criterion used for all rows. Let $g=(p-1)/2$ and $\pi=\zeta_p-1$. There are one or two places of $M$ over $p$, each with ramification $p-1$ over the corresponding unramified $H_i$. The Jacobian of $T(T-1)=UX^p$, $T=(1+Y)/2$, has isogeny $\lambda=1-\tau_*$ for $\tau(X,T)=(\zeta_p X,T)$. Its rational kernel is generated by $R=[(0,0)-\infty]$ in $(X,T)$ coordinates, by the divisor of $T$ and the eigenvalues on the $g$ differentials $X^{j-1}dX/Y$. Kummer evaluation uses $T$, and $\delta(R)=-U^{g+1}$. All global classes are represented by units because their valuations are divisible by $p$ and $\operatorname{Cl}(M)[p]=0$. Any such class locally represented by $1+c\pi^p$ at all places over $p$ is globally trivial: the local Kummer equation after putting the root equal to $1+\pi Z$ reduces to $Z^p-Z-\bar c$, so the resulting abelian extension is everywhere unramified.

The formal Picard group of the integral double-cover model has $\lambda:F^m\simeq F^{m+1}$ at positive depths with graded entries $-j$, $1\le j\le g$. Indeed the automorphism reduces to the identity on the special fibre and the linear part after dividing by $\pi$ is invertible. Formal coordinates are obtained from $g$ smooth sections with mutually distinct abscissas; one can choose $Z_i=2,3,\ldots,g,\bar x$, $r_i=(Z_i^2-1)/(4U^{1/p})$, $Y_i=Z_i^p$ in each residue field, whose degree is three or six. The evaluation matrix is Vandermonde up to nonzero column scalars. Initial displacement of the putative point has depth $(p-1)(v_p(S)-1)$ and each differential coordinate has the same nonzero rational multiple of $W/Y_0$ as its leading residue whenever $W$ is nonzero there. The statement with zero on a component is valid as a congruence as well.

In the two-place case write $q_i=(N_{H_i/\mathbb Q_p}(U)^{p-1}-1)/p\bmod p$. The two nonzero opposite digits in the table ensure simultaneous global division. In detail, a global lift exists by detection when a local preimage exists at each place. Its differences from the prescribed positive-depth lifts are $t_iR$. Applying the Fermat quotient to the absolute norm of an ordinary-unit representative of the lift's Kummer class gives $(g+1)(p-1)\sum_i t_i q_i=0\bmod p$. The norm down to $H_i$ of $1+c\pi^p$ is 1 modulo $p^2$. The two offsets therefore agree, and subtracting their common multiple of $R$ realizes both prescribed lifts globally. For a single place rationality of the kernel suffices.

After $(p-1)(v_p(S)-1)-1$ divisions the point is global, lies at formal depth at least one everywhere over $p$, and has graded coordinates $v_j=c_0jW/Y_0$, $c_0\in\mathbb F_p^\times$. Its Kummer class is locally represented by $1+\pi^p L\bmod\pi^{p+1}$ everywhere. Detection forces zero absolute residue trace at each place because a $p$th power at that depth has digit $b^p-b$. If $\pi b_i$ are the section displacements, the actual digit is $L=\sum_i\kappa_i(b_i^p-r_i^{p-1}b_i)$ with $\kappa_i=U/(Y_iT_i)$. Pairing the graded vector with the coefficients of $(1-(1+4U^{1/p}X)^g)/(2X)$ gives
$$\operatorname{Tr}(L)=c_0\operatorname{Tr}\big(W(U/Y_0^3)^{1/p}\big).$$
Indeed $r_i\kappa_i^{1/p}=(Z_i-1)/(2Z_i)$, trace is Frobenius invariant, and differentiating $[1-(1+4U^{1/p}X)^g]/2$ gives $U^{1/p}(1+4U^{1/p}X)^{g-1}$ in characteristic $p$. The derivations of formal division, detection and this trace formula are given in full in the linked proofs. Every row has a nonzero trace in at least one place. This gives the contradiction and proves the stated theorem.
