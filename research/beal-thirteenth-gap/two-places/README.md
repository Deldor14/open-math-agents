# Two-place cyclotomic descent for (13,13,17) and (13,13,23)

Deldor14 · 28 November 2026 · Written proof for mathematical review

**Claim.** For $p=17$ and for $p=23$, every primitive integer solution of $a^{13}+b^{13}=c^p$ satisfies $abc=0$. We use a norm constraint to show that formal divisions at two distinct primes above $p$ can be made by a single global point at every step.

## The point and its two disks

Suppose $abc\ne0$. Let $D=a+b$, $z=\zeta_{13}$, $\theta=z+z^{-1}$, $H=\mathbb Q(\theta)$, $M=H(\xi)$, $\xi=\zeta_p$. Best–Dahmen–Freitas Theorem 1.2 excludes $13\mid D$. Their (4.3) and §§4.2–4.3 give $D=v^p$ and $a+bz=\beta^p$; their auxiliary pairs are $(17,103)$ and $(23,139)$. [Best–Dahmen–Freitas](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.12092v1#S4.SS3)

If $p\nmid D$, put $t=a/D\bmod p^2$. Then $t+(1-t)z$ is a $p$th power. In the cyclotomic quotient modulo $p^2$, raising it to $p^{12}-1$ must give 1, since residue degrees divide 12 and the element is a unit (the residue of $z$ is not rational). Testing all $t$ gives only $0,1$. If $p\mid D$, then $p^2\mid D$ and the $p$th power $a+bz$ is congruent to $a(1-z)$ with $a$ a rational unit. The same power of $1-z$ is nonscalar in this quotient for both $p$. Thus $p\nmid D$ and $S=ab/D^2$ has finite $e=v_p(S)\ge2$. A second implementation checks necessary Frobenius congruences in both charts.

In $H$ put $d_1=2-\theta$, $d_2=4-\theta^2$, $U=(\theta+1)(\theta+2)$, $Y_0=2\theta+3$ and $W=6+\theta-2\theta^2$. The two conjugates $G_i=\sigma_i(\beta\bar\beta)/v^2$, $\sigma_i(z)=z^i$, satisfy $G_i^p=1-d_iS$. Set
$$X=G_1/G_2^2,\qquad Y=(Y_0-d_2 S)/(1-d_2 S).$$
The identity $(Y_0-d_2 S)^2=(1-d_2 S)^2+4U(1-d_1 S)$ puts $P=(X,Y)$ on $C:Y^2=1+4UX^p$ with reference point $P_0=(1,Y_0)$. Both $U$ and $Y_0$ are global units by their sextic norms. At each of the two places of $H$ over $p$ the field is unramified cubic, and $G_i$ reduces to 1. Expanding the $p$th powers gives $X-1\equiv p^{e-1}Ws_0\pmod{p^e}$ with $s_0=S/p^e\bmod p\in\mathbb F_p^\times$. Both $W$ and $Y_0$ are nonzero in both residue fields.

## A norm condition on the two local lifts

Write $g=(p-1)/2$, $T=(Y+1)/2$, $J=\operatorname{Jac}(C)$, $\tau(X,T)=(\xi X,T)$ and $\lambda=1-\tau_*$. Let $R=[(0,0)-\infty]$ in $(X,T)$ coordinates. The kernel and dual kernel of $\lambda$ are generated by the rational point $R$: the divisor of $T$ is $pR$ in divisor notation, the branch difference is nonzero, and the eigenvalues on the $g$ holomorphic differentials and their conjugates give degree $p$ for $\lambda$. The dual is $1-\tau_*^{-1}$. Evaluation of $T$ gives an injective Kummer map $\delta:J(K)/\lambda J(K)\hookrightarrow K^\times/K^{\times p}$ over $M$ and its completions. This follows by pairing the connecting homomorphism with $R$ in the dual kernel. [Schaefer, descent using functions](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.08325)

Its value on the torsion generator is
$$\delta(R)=-U^{g+1}\quad\bmod M^{\times p}.$$
Indeed the leading coefficient of $T$ at the zero, using parameter $X$, is $-U$ since $T(T-1)=U X^p$. At infinity use parameter $s=X^g/T$. Then $Xs^2\to U^{-1}$ and $Ts^p\to U^{-g}$. Evaluation on this divisor uses the quotient of the two leading coefficients; changing either parameter multiplies the result by a $p$th power. This evaluation rule follows from moving the divisor and Weil reciprocity with local tame symbols. Signs are themselves $p$th powers here.

All global Kummer classes have valuations divisible by $p$, by the equation and the fact that $U$ is a unit. At closed points take norms: a positive valuation of $T$ gives $v(T-1)=0$, and a negative valuation gives $v(T-1)=v(T)$, so $p\mid v(T)$ in both cases. The ordinary class group of $M$ has trivial $p$-part by the unconditional Aoki–Fukuda computation below. Therefore each global Kummer class has an ordinary unit representative.

Put $\pi=\xi-1$. At each of the two $p$-adic places $k_i$ of $M$, the ramification degree over $H_i$ is $p-1$ and the residue field is $\mathbb F_{p^3}$. A global Kummer class lying locally at *both* places in the image of $1+\pi^p\mathcal O_{k_i}$ is trivial. Adjoining the $p$th root of the unit representative gives an abelian extension unramified away from $p$ by the unit condition. At each place over $p$ the polynomial $((1+\pi Z)^p-1)/\pi^p-c$ reduces to $Z^p-Z-\bar c$ for a local representative $1+c\pi^p$. It acquires simple roots in an unramified extension. The field $M$ is totally imaginary and has class number prime to $p$, proving the assertion by class field theory.

Let $F_i^m$ denote the depth-$m$ formal subgroup of the relative Picard scheme in the integral double-cover model at $k_i$. The local properties used here are
$$\lambda:F_i^m\xrightarrow{\sim} F_i^{m+1}\ (m\ge1),\qquad \delta(F_i^1)\subseteq (1+\pi^p\mathcal O_{k_i})k_i^{\times p}.$$
For detail, glue $T^2-T=UX^p$ with the infinity chart $v^2-z'^{g+1}v=Uz'$. The special fibre is geometrically integral because $T(T-1)/\bar U$ has a simple zero. The formal Picard group is smooth by vanishing of $H^2(\mathcal O)$, and the integral dualizing differentials are $X^{j-1}dX/Y$, $1\le j\le g$, by adjunction. In each residue field choose $d=U^{1/p}$ and $Z_i=2,3,\ldots,g,\bar\theta$, giving $g$ distinct $r_i=(Z_i^2-1)/(4d)$ and $Y_i=Z_i^p$, with $T_iY_i\ne0$. Their smooth Hensel sections give Abel coordinates by the invertible differential $(r_i^{j-1}/Y_i)_{j,i}$. In these formal coordinates all coefficients of $\lambda$ are divisible by $\pi$ because $\tau$ reduces identically; in differential coordinates the linear part of $\lambda/\pi$ has entries $-j$ on its diagonal modulo $\pi$. This gives the first property by formal inversion. For displacements $w_i\in\pi\mathcal O_{k_i}$ the curve equation gives $T_i(w_i)/T_i\in1+\pi^p\mathcal O_{k_i}$ by the binomial theorem and $v_\pi(p)=p-1$. Their product gives the second property. [Integral formal argument for one place](https://github.com/Deldor14/open-math-agents/tree/c668e382272096b322b6bd376911aa89288bb304/research/beal-thirteenth-gap/split-eleven) · [Original degree-seven method](https://github.com/Deldor14/open-math-agents/tree/b2d65981681cf44455709e953d70fe393a606e83/research/beal-septic-local/seven-eleven)

**Simultaneous division lemma.** Suppose that a global $A\in J(M)$ has prescribed local preimages $B_i\in F_i^1$ under $\lambda$ at both places. Define $q_i=(N_{H_i/\mathbb Q_p}(U)^{p-1}-1)/p\pmod p$. If $q_1=-q_2\ne0$, the two prescribed preimages are the localizations of one global preimage.

*Proof.* Both local Kummer values of $A$ are trivial. The detection result gives a global preimage $B'$. There are $t_i\in\mathbb F_p$ such that $B'=B_i+t_iR$ locally. Take a global unit $u$ representing $\delta(B')$. In each $k_i$ this is $(-U^{g+1})^{t_i}v_i^p(1+c_i\pi^p)$, with $v_i$ a local unit. Its global norm is $\pm1$. The norm of $1+c_i\pi^p$ down to $H_i$ belongs to $1+p^2\mathcal O_{H_i}$: the product of conjugates is 1 modulo $\pi^p$ and valuations in $H_i$ are multiples of $p-1$ in the $\pi$ normalization. Its norm down to $\mathbb Q_p$ is therefore 1 modulo $p^2$. The local $p$th powers have rational local norms which are $p$th powers. Apply the additive homomorphism on rational $p$-adic units $l(x)=(x^{p-1}-1)/p\bmod p$ to the product of the two local norms. Because $U\in H_i$, its norm from $k_i$ to $H_i$ is $U^{p-1}$. Thus
$$0=(g+1)(p-1)(t_1 q_1+t_2 q_2)\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_p.$$
The scalar factor is nonzero, hence $t_1=t_2$. Subtract their common multiple of $R$ from $B'$. This proves the lemma.

## The boundary trace and finite data

For $A=[P-P_0]$ the initial vector at each place has depth $(p-1)(e-1)$ and all its entries are the same nonzero rational residue times $W/Y_0$, by evaluation of the differentials at $P_0$. Divide simultaneously $(p-1)(e-1)-1$ times. At every step both formal preimages exist in $F_i^1$, and the preceding lemma makes them global together. For the resulting global point $B$, its differential vector at each place at depth 1 has entries $v_j=c_0 j W/Y_0$ with $c_0\in\mathbb F_p^\times$, by the diagonal graded division map. Its Kummer value lies in $1+\pi^p\mathcal O$ at both places and is globally trivial by detection.

For completeness, in a single section chart with $w_i=\pi b_i\bmod\pi^2$, the product of the $T$-ratios is $1+\pi^p L\bmod\pi^{p+1}$ with
$$L=\sum_i\kappa_i(b_i^p-r_i^{p-1}b_i),\qquad\kappa_i=U/(Y_iT_i).$$
This uses $p/\pi^{p-1}\equiv-1$. Triviality forces $\operatorname{Tr}(L)=0$ since the boundary coefficient of a $p$th power is $b^p-b$. On the other hand $Y_i(\kappa_i^{1/p}-r_i^{p-1}\kappa_i)=H_1(r_i)$ with $H_1(x)=(1-(1+4dx)^g)/(2x)$, by $r_i\kappa_i^{1/p}=(Z_i-1)/(2Z_i)$. Using Frobenius invariance of trace and $v_j=\sum_i r_i^{j-1}b_i/Y_i$, evaluation against the coefficients of $H_1$ gives
$$\operatorname{Tr}(L)=c_0\operatorname{Tr}\bigl((W/Y_0)(xH_1)'(1)\bigr)
=c_0\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(W(U/Y_0^3)^{1/p}\bigr).$$
Here $(xH_1)'=d(1+4dx)^{g-1}$ in characteristic $p$ and $(Y_0^{1/p})^2=1+4d$.

The polynomials listed below are the two monic cubic factors of the sextic $y^6+y^5-5y^4-4y^3+6y^2+3y-1$ modulo $p^2$. The norm of $U$ in each row is the product of the cubic evaluated at $-1$ and $-2$. The last column is the boundary trace divided by $c_0$.

| $p$ | cubic modulo $p^2$ | norm modulo $p^2$ | $q_i$ | boundary trace |
|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 17 | $y^3+30y^2+288y+258$ | 206 | 7 | 3 |
| 17 | $y^3+260y^2+288y+28$ | 94 | 10 | 5 |
| 23 | $y^3+245y^2+528y+283$ | 330 | 9 | 17 |
| 23 | $y^3+285y^2+528y+243$ | 210 | 14 | 0 |

The quotients are nonzero opposites for both exponents. Each exponent has a place with nonzero boundary trace. Thus both cases contradict global triviality of the Kummer value of $B$.
