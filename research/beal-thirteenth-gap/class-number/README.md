# Removing GRH from the primitive equation x¹³ + y¹³ = z⁷

Deldor14 · 28 November 2026 · Computational proof submitted for mathematical review

For integers a,b,c with gcd(a,b,c)=1, the equation a¹³+b¹³=c⁷ implies abc=0. There is no bound on the bases, and either sign is allowed. Best, Dahmen and Freitas obtained this conclusion subject to GRH. We use their unconditional reductions in §§2–4, certify the class group needed for the rank in §5.2, and give an independently reproducible Chabauty calculation at 5. This settles one exponent triple in the Beal problem. [Best–Dahmen–Freitas](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.12092v1)

## 1. The class group

Let ρ³+ρ²−4ρ+1=0, K=ℚ(ρ), s=ρ²−ρ+1. Put F=ℚ(t), t⁷=2ρ, so t²¹+2t¹⁴−16t⁷+8=0. The field F has degree n=21, r₁=3 real embeddings and absolute discriminant Δ=2¹⁸7²¹13¹⁴. The field proof and maximal-order calculation are in the preceding descent note. Both enumerations here independently initialize the maximal order. We prove **Cl(F)=1**. [Descent note and verifiers](https://github.com/Deldor14/open-math-agents/tree/e4389d639d69d9954c0f323dda380149cf24f052/research/beal-thirteenth-gap/selmer-seven)

Take T=2²². For each prime ideal with norm N and each integer m≥1 with Nᵐ<T, form the summand

    4 log(N) (1 − log(Nᵐ)/log T) / (21 (1+Nᵐ)).

Let S denote their sum. The unconditional Corollary 5.2 of Belabas, Diaz y Diaz and Friedman implies that the prime ideals of norm <T generate Cl(F) if S+A>0, where

    A = γ + log(4π) + 1/7 − (log Δ)/21 − (π²/4 + (log 4)/7)/log T.

This is their inequality (23) divided by 21, with r₁/n=1/7. The result in §5 of their paper is unconditional. [Belabas–Diaz y Diaz–Friedman, Corollary 5.2](https://www.math.u-bordeaux.fr/~kbelabas/pub/OnBach.pdf)

The following are lower bounds, rounded down. All three denominators are 10¹².

| Quantity | Lower numerator |
|---|---:|
| S | 1,189,955,252,169 |
| A | −1,174,553,533,666 |
| S+A | **15,401,718,503** |

For each integer v, the bit length of v²⁵⁶ gives j=⌊256 log₂(v)⌋ exactly. We bound log₂(v) between j/256 and (j+1)/256 and round each positive weighted summand down. For A, we use γ>H₁₀₂₄−log(1025) and 223/71<π<22/7. Rational logarithms are reduced to [1,2] and enclosed with the positive series for twice atanh((x−1)/(x+1)) and a geometric tail. The whole sign test uses integer and rational arithmetic.

For every rational prime q<T, the principal-ideal checker decomposes q in the maximal order, admitting all residue degrees f such that qᶠ<T. For each resulting ideal 𝔭, it obtains a candidate generator g and checks equality of the exact Hermite normal forms of (g) and 𝔭. This verifies **296,336 individual ideal equalities**. An independent enumeration factors the defining polynomial modulo small primes and counts its distinct roots at larger primes, with separate decomposition at 2,7,13. These are the only divisors of its polynomial discriminant. Both counts agree at **each of 88,351 distinct norms**. The unconditional generation bound and the checked ideal equalities give the claim. PARI's provisional relation data are used to find g. The proof uses the individual equalities in the maximal order.

## 2. The global descent

Set C to be the smooth projective model of Y²=X⁷+4⁶s⁷/ρ², with Jacobian J. Let

    X₀=4s,   Y₀=2⁶s⁴(ρ²+ρ−5),   P=(X₀,Y₀),   D=[P−∞].

The descent note shows that once Cl(F)[2]=0 the 2-Selmer group of J/K is generated by the image of D. Only its upper bound and nonzero image are needed here. Briefly, unramifiedness outside 2,7,13 puts every Selmer element into the norm kernel on S-units modulo squares, since Cl(F)=1. Its dimension is 12. The dyadic and 13-adic local Kummer spaces have dimensions 9 and 3. With the global space their joint rank is 23, leaving at most one dimension. The class of D is detected locally. In particular rank J(K)≤1. Below we compute a nonzero 5-adic logarithm of 9D, giving equality. [Global elements, local generators and two localization calculations](https://github.com/Deldor14/open-math-agents/tree/e4389d639d69d9954c0f323dda380149cf24f052/research/beal-thirteenth-gap/selmer-seven)

## 3. Three residue disks

Embed K into ℚ₅ by ρ≡2 modulo 5. This simple root lifts to ρ≡12 modulo 25. Then X₀≡7 and Y₀≡−6 modulo 25. The curve has good reduction with equation y²=x⁷+3 modulo 5. The septic has a simple root 3 modulo 5, lifting to a root θ₅ in ℚ₅. Project the global descent class to this degree-one component. A finite nonbranch point with coordinate x has class x−θ₅. The class for P projects to a square because X₀−θ₅≡4 modulo 5. The Selmer bound therefore requires the projection for every K-point to be a square.

The affine residue points are (1,±2), (2,±1), (3,0). At x≡1 the difference is a nonsquare unit. In the disk at (3,0), the quotient (x⁷+4⁶s⁷/ρ²)/(x−θ₅) reduces to 7·3⁶≡3, a nonsquare, so x−θ₅ is a nonsquare modulo local squares. There is no global branch point since the septic is irreducible over K. Thus the only surviving disks are those of (2,1), (2,4) and ∞, occupied by −P, P and ∞ respectively.

## 4. An annihilating differential

Write z=x−X₀ and use the basis ωᵢ=xⁱ dx/(2y), 0≤i≤2. We compute the first nonzero digit of the formal logarithm of 9D in this basis. The model and marked point modulo 25 are also the reductions of y²=x⁷−823507 and (7,−6) over ℚ. Computation with this lift is legitimate since multiplication and translation in the good Jacobian commute with reduction modulo 25.

Here are details of a computation directly at P. Let y(z) be the branch with y(0)=Y₀. Choose a monic quartic h and a polynomial g of degree ≤7 such that g+h y(z) vanishes through degree 11. Expanding y(z) and solving the four high-coefficient equations gives, in ascending powers of z modulo 25,

    h = (15,20,20,6,1),   g = (15,5,20,21,0,17,21,14).

The four pivots are units at 5, so the construction lifts over ℤ₅. The function g+h y has pole order 15 at ∞ and at least twelve zeros counted with multiplicity at P. The residual effective divisor has degree three. Its hyperelliptic conjugate represents 12D. Its monic abscissa polynomial u₁₂ is obtained from the norm identity

    h² f − g² = z¹² u₁₂,       u₁₂ ≡ z³ + 15z² + 5z + 15  (mod 25),

where f=y². Modulo 5 both g and h are divisible by z³, and (g+h y)/z³ has norm up to sign z⁹ and value 2 at −P. Its divisor is 9(P)−9(∞), giving 9D=0 on the special fiber. The residual divisor therefore specializes after conjugation to 3(P). Indeed it is uniquely represented by that effective degree-three divisor because 3(P) contains no hyperelliptic pair. The monic polynomial for 3(P) is u₃=z³; its ordinate polynomial v₃ has first three Taylor coefficients (4,1,2) modulo 5.

The coefficients of the monic cubic u are local parameters on the Jacobian near 3(P). The Abel map on the symmetric cube is étale there, since 3(P) is nonspecial, and x is a local parameter at P. In this chart du=(u₁₂−u₃)/5 modulo 5 equals 3z²+z+3. The pairing of this tangent with ωᵢ is minus the coefficient of z² in xⁱ du/(2v₃) reduced modulo z³. To see the formula at distinct abscissas xⱼ, differentiate u(xⱼ)=0 and sum xⱼⁱ dxⱼ/(2yⱼ). Lagrange interpolation identifies this sum with the stated coefficient. Both expressions extend regularly to the repeated-point chart, since v₃ is a unit modulo u₃. Translation identifies the tangent with the reduction of log(9D)/5; the formal logarithm is the identity to first order on 5ℤ₅. We obtain

    log(9D)/5 ≡ (4,0,3) (mod 5).

A second calculation using Cantor addition computes 3D and 12D on the rational lift and gives the same vector. In particular D has infinite order. As J(K) has rank one, all its logarithms belong to this one-dimensional ℚ₅-space. Its primitive integral direction is annihilated modulo 5 by (3,0,1). At least one coordinate of that direction is a unit, so this covector lifts to an integral annihilator of the exact line. The resulting differential reduces to (3+x²)dx/(2y). At both finite surviving residues, 3+x²≡2. At infinity the leading coefficient of x² is 1, so the differential is nonvanishing there too.

For completeness, in a disk where the reduction of the annihilating differential is nonzero, take an integral local parameter t vanishing at a known point. Its integral has leading term a₀t with a₀ a unit. For t∈5ℤ₅, t≠0, each term aⱼtʲ⁺¹/(j+1), j≥1, has strictly larger valuation. Thus each such disk contains at most one K-point. This proves C(K)={P,−P,∞}. [McCallum–Poonen on Chabauty and Coleman](https://math.mit.edu/~poonen/papers/chabauty.pdf)

## 5. Returning to the Fermat equation

Best–Dahmen–Freitas, Theorem 1.2, deal with 13 dividing a+b. In the remaining case, their unit sieve in §4 leaves e=1 for p=7. Their formulas (3.2)–(3.4) send a putative primitive solution to a finite point on C, with X/X₀=z₁/z₂⁴, z₂⁷=a+b≠0 and z₁⁷=F(a,b). The two finite points have X=X₀. Hence F(a,b)=(a+b)⁴. In their quartic F, the coefficient of ρ² in the basis 1,ρ,ρ² is a²b². The right hand side is rational, forcing ab=0. Together these cases give precisely the primitive trivial solutions with a+b=0 or ab=0 and c determined by the equation. [Best–Dahmen–Freitas, §§2–4](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.12092v1#S3)

    python3 replay.py principal.txt --gp /path/to/gp --jobs 3
    gp -qf -s 256M factor_counts.gp > factors.txt
    python3 inequality.py principal.txt factors.txt
    python3 chabauty.py
    gp -qf cantor.gp

The same directory contains the replay output. ChabautyFinite.lean compiles with Lean 4.33.1 and Mathlib and checks every coefficient of the modular norm identity and the finite disk sieve.
