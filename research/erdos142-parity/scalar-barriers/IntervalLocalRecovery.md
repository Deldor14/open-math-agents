# Nine local steps recover the extremal density scale in sparse interval samples

Let S be obtained by retaining each of the N sites of an interval independently with probability p. Form the unordered hypergraph of its nonconstant three-term progressions, erasing the positions, the order of the sites, and the identity of the midpoint of every edge. Write d=Np² and suppose d→∞ with log d=o(log N). There is a randomized radius-nine rule on this unlabelled incidence structure, with parameters N and d, that always returns a progression-free subset and has expected size at least

    c Np r₃(⌈d⌉)/⌈d⌉.

The constant is absolute. Thus it achieves a constant fraction of the expected optimum by the [effective-length upper estimate](https://github.com/Deldor14/open-math-agents/blob/68cd54337b3e1a61aeca710263c2e0f012292c8c/research/erdos142-hinge/RothEffectiveLength.md). This is an existence result for a local rule: one finite progression-free alphabet can be precomputed for each d. Using Behrend's explicit alphabet instead gives expected density exp(−O(√log d)). Radius counts vertex-to-vertex steps across a common hyperedge. Independent randomness at vertices suffices.

The analogous unlabelled rule on a sparse cyclic host can only obtain O(√(log d/d)) expected density even at certain growing radii. The interval has a boundary: its vertex degrees encode the distance to the centre, up to reflection. The argument below recovers this distance more accurately than a single degree measurement. [Cyclic comparison](LocalSamplingBarrier.md)

## The finite typed branching experiment

For analysis only, give a vertex its actual integer position x in [1,N]. In an ideal rooted tree, for each nonconstant arithmetic-progression edge through x in the full interval attach an independent Poisson(p²) number of copies, each introducing both other sites as new vertices with their correct positions. Repeat independently at every occurrence. The branching mean is at most d. At any fixed radius r the rooted typed neighborhood law, conditioned on retaining a given root x, differs from the corresponding ideal law in total variation by O_r(N^(−1/20)) for sufficiently large N under the stated hypothesis on d. The bound is uniform in x. The approximation also holds as N→∞ for each fixed d. The continuous interval type and fixed-parameter local limit also occur in the parallel [critical-limit proof](https://github.com/Deldor14/open-math-agents/blob/ab0d4df5814c6c4e4ce2d7e2b5b8c785096be6b7/research/erdos142-hinge/RothCriticalLimit.md).

Here are details allowing growing d. Truncate the ideal ball at B=⌊N^(1/16)⌋ vertices. Its expected size is O_r((1+d)^r), so the discarded probability is N^(−1/16+o(1)). Call a tree with actual positions valid when all its sites are distinct and its only internal progressions are the prescribed edges. A collision or unintended edge is witnessed by the union of at most three paths from the root, with s≤3r edges and at most three marked nodes. For each of finitely many such forms and choices of midpoints the expected number of witnesses is at most a constant times (p²)^s N^(s−1). For specified coordinates the expected number of copies is (p²)^s by the Poisson factorial moments, also for repeated edge choices. The tree equations with the fixed root leave s free variables. The collision or extra triple supplies one independent equation over the rationals, by the [private-coordinate argument](LooseForestRows.lean). Fixing all its free integer coordinates leaves at most one assignment of its pivot coordinates; interval restrictions and parities can only reduce the count. The total invalid probability is O_r((1+d)^(3r)/N).

For each valid tree with e edges, the ideal probability at these exact coordinates is p^(2e) exp(−p² Σ g(v)), the sum over interior vertices, where g(v) is the number of incident full-interval edges. Distinct position labels remove any automorphism multiplicity. In the sample, after retaining the other 2e sites, there are O(B²) individually forbidden external sites, and an ordinary graph of forbidden outside pairs. Its edge count differs from Σg(v) by O(B²) and its maximum degree is at most 3B. These statements follow from the same two-anchor linear equations as in the cyclic comparison, now over the integers with possible parity restrictions. Harris and Janson bound the probability that these extra edges are absent by exp(−p² Σg(v)+O(B²Np³+B²p+BNp⁴)). The relative comparison is uniform and summable over valid trees with at most B vertices. The bound on the ideal complement then controls total variation including all nontrees. Adding independent vertex marks preserves the comparison.

## Estimating the folded position

Write t=|x/N−1/2|. The full degree is

    g(x)=min(x−1,N−x)+⌊(x−1)/2⌋+⌊(N−x)/2⌋ = N(1−t)+O(1)

uniformly. In a rooted ball the incident edges leading away from the parent are called child edges. Split those edges into two classes with probability 1/2 each. On the ideal tree the split is independent between child edges and at distinct vertices. This can be done with independent vertex marks: classify a child edge by whether the smaller of its two new vertex marks lies below 1−1/√2. The estimate at a child uses only the marks of its descendants, so it is independent of its own mark. The split depends on the root used for this local computation. Each candidate vertex runs the same rooted procedure. A ball that is not a loose tree can use the fallback of rejecting the vertex.

Use one class for a preliminary measurement s=1−2D/d, where D is its child-edge count. Put δ₀=1/1000, σ=δ₀/10. The probability of |s−t|>σ is ≤exp(−c d) for all sufficiently large d,N, uniformly in x, by Poisson concentration (constants below may depend on δ₀). The second class is an independent Poisson process conditional on x.

For 1≤j≤8 put Iⱼ=[1/8+4jδ₀,3/8−4jδ₀]. Define an estimate Zⱼ at radius j. Uniformly in types t∈Iⱼ and conditional on any preliminary s in the good range |s−t|≤σ, it satisfies

    |E Zⱼ−t| ≤ Cⱼ(N^(−1)+exp(−cⱼd)),
    E[(Zⱼ−t)²] ≤ Cⱼ(d^(−j)+N^(−2)+exp(−cⱼd)).                           (2)

All estimates lie in [−1,1]. For j=1 use 1−2D'/d from the second class, clipped to [−1,1]. The unclipped mean differs from t by O(1/N); clipping changes the mean by exponentially little by the Poisson tail and bounded second moment. Its variance is O(1/d).

For j≥2, examine the second-class child edges. Keep an edge when the preliminary measurements of its two children fall respectively in [s−3δ₀,s−δ₀] and [s+δ₀,s+3δ₀], in either order. Average (Z_(j−1)(u)+Z_(j−1)(w))/2 over the kept edges. Use zero if none is kept. The computation requires radius j, including the preliminary measurements at the children.

With probability 1−O(exp(−c d)) all inspected child measurements are good, and there are at least c' d kept edges. Indeed their expected number of bad measurements is O(d exp(−cd)). The actual midpoint edges at x with distance between 1.5δ₀N and 2.5δ₀N from x to either endpoint are always available for these interior types. There are at least c'N such edges before Poisson sampling. The second-class copies from that range have Poisson mean at least c'd. All are kept when the measurements are good, by the margins in the test.

On this event the two child types of every kept edge lie in I_(j−1), strictly on opposite sides of t, and each differs from t by at most 3δ₀+2σ. Three interval sites with folded positions in [l,u], 0<l and u<3l, in a progression must lie on the same side of the centre: opposite-signed endpoints would put the middle point at folded distance ≤(u−l)/2<l. Therefore, on every kept edge the root really is the midpoint and its t is the arithmetic mean of the two folded child types. No algorithmic midpoint information was used. [FoldedInterval.lean](FoldedInterval.lean) verifies the one-sided band, the strict median in a nonconstant progression, and the later cell-index conclusion.

Condition on the root measurement, all second-class edge types, and all their child measurements. The second-class estimation subtrees at the different children are independent conditional on these data. Induction gives the stated uniform bias for each kept average. Their conditional variances are bounded by the preceding mean-square bound and dividing by at least c'd gives (2), after adding the square of the uniform bias. On the exceptional event bounded estimates cost O(exp(−c d)) in both bounds. This proves (2) on the ideal tree. The actual ball can contribute an additional O(N^(−1/20)) to any bounded unconditional error estimate by the preceding total variation comparison. In particular for d=N^o(1) this is smaller than every fixed negative power of d for large N.

## A feasible selection using the estimate

Put q=⌈100d⌉, m=⌊q/20⌋, and take a progression-free D⊂[0,m) of size r₃(m). Use centres v_j=0.22+j/q for j∈D. Declare a vertex a candidate if its preliminary measurement is in [0.19,0.31] and its Z₈ is within 1/(12q) of a centre. Finally reject every candidate lying in any fully candidate hyperedge. This final test uses radius nine and always gives an independent set.

Count a retained vertex as bad if its estimator ball is not a loose tree, its preliminary measurement is inaccurate, or has a good preliminary measurement passing the gate and |Z₈−t|>1/(36q). Whenever the gate passes with a good measurement, t∈I₈. Markov's inequality applied to (2) gives conditional bad probability O(d^(−6)) at each retained root, uniformly in its actual position (also counting the total variation error). At any root the true sampled degree, conditional on retention, has second moment O(d²) uniformly: among its O(N) possible outside pairs each shares a site with at most a constant number of other pairs. It follows from Cauchy–Schwarz that the expected number of bad-root incidences is O(Np d^(−2)). This also bounds the expected number of edges containing any bad point.

Every retained site with true folded type within 1/(18q) of a centre is a candidate unless bad. Its true type forces the gate to pass whenever its preliminary measurement is good. Even on just one side of the centre these inner intervals contain at least N|D|/(12q) sites for large N. Conversely, a good candidate has true folded type within 1/(9q) of a centre. Their whole band satisfies u<3l, so an edge of good candidates lies on one physical side and its three folded types satisfy the midpoint equation. Its cell indices therefore differ from an integer midpoint equation by at most 4/9; the integer error is zero. Freeness of D forces all three into the same physical cell. There are two physical cells per index, each of length 2N/(9q)+O(1)≤N/(4q) for large N. The number of full-interval edges contained in these cells is at most 2|D|(N/(4q))². Counting all such edges in the sample costs at most p³ times this number. Deleting all sites in fully candidate edges loses at most three sites per edge. Altogether the expected output is at least

    Np |D|/q · [1/12−3d/(8q)] − O(Np d^(−2)).

Here |D|/q≥c r₃(⌈d⌉)/⌈d⌉, and Behrend's bound absorbs the additive error. The construction uses no coordinates or type marks as inputs. It still works with any explicitly supplied alphabet having density much larger than d^(−2). [Behrend's construction](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.32.12.331)

## Concentration of the local output

The size bound also holds with probability tending to one, with a possibly smaller absolute constant. The typed-tree estimate applies to two distinct prescribed roots conditioned on both being retained, with a product of two independent ideal balls. To see this, use their disjoint union as the good forest. In the witness count there are now two fixed vertices. If a collision or additional midpoint equation were dependent modulo the tree rows and both root-coordinate rows, the two root coefficients would sum to zero. A nonzero multiple of the difference of the prescribed root values makes the equality impossible. If that multiple vanishes, the forest support argument excludes the dependence, except for an already prescribed edge. Thus the witness bound is unchanged, allowing two components and up to three paths in all. Any encounter with the other root or between the two balls is included. The outside-pair computation now uses the union of the interiors. The relative comparison gives mass at least 1−O_r(N^(−1/20)) on matching pairs of balls with disjoint labels; extra cross-edges on their union may be forbidden for this lower comparison. This suffices for total variation. The marks in the two ideal trees are independent.

For a bounded vertex indicator using a fixed radius, the difference between the joint conditional expectation at distinct retained sites and the product of the respective single-root conditional expectations is therefore O_r(N^(−1/20)). For its total count Y the normalized variance Var(Y)/(Np)² is O_r((Np)^(−1)+N^(−1/20)). The expected selected density in the preceding theorem is at least N^(−o(1)), so Chebyshev's inequality gives concentration relative to that lower scale.

## A variational characterization using a fixed-depth tree

There is a version whose definition involves no extremal set or alphabet. Generate the continuous interval tree with a uniform root x∈[0,1]. At a vertex x place midpoint edges (x−s,x+s) at Poisson intensity d ds for 0<s<min(x,1−x), and endpoint edges (x±s,x±2s) at the same intensity for each sign and each s keeping the three types in [0,1]. Generate new subtrees independently. Then forget all positions and midpoint marks. Give vertices independent uniform marks.

Let V₉(d) be the supremum over all measurable radius-eight candidate rules of the probability that the root is a candidate and belongs to no all-candidate edge. This is a bounded functional of the unlabelled marked depth-nine tree. For every sufficiently large real d,

    c r₃(⌈d⌉)/⌈d⌉ ≤ V₉(d) ≤ C r₃(⌈d⌉)/⌈d⌉.

For the upper bound take N→∞ first at fixed d. After types are forgotten, the discrete ideal tree converges in total variation at each fixed depth to this continuous experiment. One can couple the finite uniform root by rounding, and use homogeneous Poisson processes for potential integer steps s/N by rounding continuous steps into intervals of width 1/N. For each vertex the support cutoffs differ by O_r(1/N) throughout a fixed-depth coupling: errors in the parent coordinate and rounded steps grow by a fixed constant per level. Poisson atoms within that distance of a cutoff have total expected number O_(r,d)(1/N); the expected ball size is bounded for fixed d,r. This couples the unmarked shapes, then uses the same independent vertex marks. Each repaired rule is feasible on the finite sample, so the upper estimate for its expected optimum bounds its probability in the limit. The comparison is uniform over bounded observables in total variation. The lower construction has constants uniform for large d; (2) on the ideal tree, or the same finite argument followed by N→∞ for each such d, gives the reverse bound. The logarithmic deficit −log V₉(d) consequently differs from log(⌈d⌉/r₃(⌈d⌉)) by O(1). Determining this variational optimum explicitly is still open.
