# The first two powers of a split exponent in septic Fermat

**Written proof for review.** Let $p\ge11$ be prime, $p\equiv\pm1\pmod7$, $K=\mathbb Q(\zeta_7)^+$ and $M=K(\zeta_p)$. If $p\nmid h(M)$, a nontrivial primitive integer solution of $a^7+b^7=c^p$ cannot have $v_p(ab)=2$. If $p\mid ab$ then $p^3\mid ab$. There is no bound on $p$ or on the bases in this criterion.

For each of the 328 primes in `exponents.txt`, every putative positive primitive solution $a^7+b^7=C^N$ with $p\mid N$ must have $p^3\mid ab$. The previous entry argument gives $p\mid ab$ for all these primes. The conclusion here is a divisibility condition. The 606 complete exclusions for different, inert primes are in the companion paper. ## The three local lifts

Suppose $p\mid ab$. As in §1 of the companion proof, $D=a+b$ and $Q=(a^7+b^7)/D$ are signed $p$th powers by coprimality and BCDF. The identity $D^6-Q=7ab(a^2+ab+b^2)^2$ gives $e=v_p(ab)\ge2$. In $K$ let $S=ab/D^2$, $d_1=2-\theta$, $d_2=4-\theta^2$, $\theta=\zeta_7+\zeta_7^{-1}$. Both $1-d_i S$ have all ideal valuations divisible by $p$ and are local $p$th powers at all three primes above $p$ since they are 1 modulo $p^2$ there. Adjoining a $p$th root to $M$ gives an everywhere unramified Kummer extension; it is trivial by the class-number hypothesis. Taking norms down to $K$ gives $G_i^p=1-d_iS$ in $K$. [BCDF II, Theorem 1.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.07062v2#S1)

Put $u=(\theta+1)(\theta+2)$, $Y_0=2\theta+3$, $W=2d_2-d_1$. The curve $Y^2=1+4uX^p$ contains $P_0=(1,Y_0)$ and $P=(G_1/G_2^2,(Y_0-d_2S)/(1-d_2S))$. At each place over $p$, $X-1\equiv p^{e-1}Ws\pmod{p^e}$, with $s=S/p^e\bmod p\in\mathbb F_p^\times$. The norms of $W,u,Y_0$ are $-49,-1,-7$, so all these values are units in each residue field.

Let $J$ be the Jacobian, $\xi=\zeta_p$, $\pi=\xi-1$, $T=(Y+1)/2$ and $\lambda=1-\tau_*$, $\tau(X,T)=(\xi X,T)$. As in the companion proof the kernel is generated by $H=[(0,0)-\infty]$ on the $(X,T)$ model, and the Kummer map is evaluation of $T$. All valuations of its global classes are multiples of $p$. A global class with local representatives in $1+\pi^p$ at **all three** places above $p$ is trivial. Indeed its cyclic $p$-extension is unramified there by the local Artin–Schreier reduction and unramified away from $p$ by the valuation condition.

At each of these places $v$, $M_v=\mathbb Q_p(\xi)$ and the formal Picard coordinates and filtrations $F_v^m$ of the companion proof apply. There are $g=(p-1)/2$ explicit smooth sections with distinct abscissas over this smaller residue field too: take $Z_i=i$ for $1\le i\le g$, $r_i=(Z_i^2-1)/(4\bar u)$, $Y_i=Z_i$, $T_i=(1+Z_i)/2$ and lift by Hensel. At $i=1$ we have $r_i=0$, $T_i=1$, which is disjoint from the divisor of $T$. The polynomial identity in §3 there holds at zero as well, since $H_{\rm poly}(0)=-2g\bar u=\bar u$ and $Y_i\kappa_i^{1/p}=\bar u$ at that point. The leading term of $\lambda/\pi$ is diagonal with entries $-j$, $1\le j\le g$ in differential coordinates. In particular $\lambda:F_v^m\to F_v^{m+1}$ is bijective for $m\ge1$.

Assume now $e=2$. The global point $A=[P-P_0]\in J(K)$ belongs to $F_v^{p-1}$ at every $v$. Write $B_{i,v}$ for the unique local lift of $A$ by $\lambda^i$ in $F_v^{p-1-i}$, $0\le i\le p-2$. We prove by induction that the three prescribed lifts are the localizations of a common global point $B_i$. This requires an argument since having some global lift leaves a separate local kernel ambiguity at each place.

Let $a\in\{1,\dots,p-1\}$ and write $\sigma_a\in\mathrm{Gal}(M/K)$ for $\xi\mapsto\xi^a$. It fixes each of the three places. In $\mathbb Z[\tau_*]\simeq\mathbb Z[\xi]$ put $U_a=1+\tau_*+\cdots+\tau_*^{a-1}$. This is a unit and $\sigma_a(\lambda)=\lambda U_a$. The integral maps preserve the formal filtrations. Uniqueness of the formal lift gives $U_a^i\sigma_a B_{i,v}=B_{i,v}$ since both sides map to the $K$-point $A$ under $\lambda^i$. On $H$, $U_a$ acts as $a$ and $\sigma_a$ fixes $H$.

If $B_{i-1}$ is global, all three localizations are $\lambda$-divisible, so the Kummer detection above gives some $D_i\in J(M)$ with $\lambda D_i=B_{i-1}$. At each place $v$ the difference $D_i-B_{i,v}$ equals $k_vH$ for some $k_v\in\mathbb F_p$. The global point
$$ E_i=\sum_{a=1}^{p-1} U_a^i\sigma_a D_i $$
has localization $(p-1)B_{i,v}$ at every $v$, because $\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}a^i=0\pmod p$ for $1\le i\le p-2$. Also the global point $\prod_{a=2}^{p-1}(1-\tau_*^a)B_{i-1}$ localizes to $pB_{i,v}$, since $\prod_{a=1}^{p-1}(1-\xi^a)=p$. Subtracting $E_i$ gives the desired $B_i$ at all three places. Start with $B_0=A$.

## The contradiction and its scope

The common global $B_{p-2}$ belongs to $F_v^1$ for each $v$. Its tangent components at each place are $c_0 j\bar W/\bar Y_0$, with $c_0\ne0$, because $(-j)^{-(p-2)}=-j$ in the residue field. Its Kummer value has a representative in $1+\pi^p$ at all places above $p$, so is globally trivial. The coefficient of $\pi^p$ modulo $\pi^{p+1}$ in each such representative must have trace zero. Here the residue field is $\mathbb F_p$ itself. The interpolation formula of the inert proof gives the coefficient $c_0\bar W\bar u/\bar Y_0^3$, nonzero at every place. This proves $e\ne2$.

For $e>2$ the number of successive divisions needed to reach depth one crosses $i=p-1$. The character sum at that step is $-1\bmod p$, and the argument above does not align the three lifts there. The stated finite application accordingly forces a cube divisor in $ab$.

The two independent relative class-number routines described in the companion paper were run on all 755 split primes $11\le p\le20000$; 328 pass, beginning at 13 and ending at 19963. The tangent verifier separately evaluates the full Kummer coefficient at each of the three roots of the cubic for all seven split primes through 100.
