# Mixed septic signatures on Beal reduction R3.H1

**Proposed results for mathematical review.** Let \(A,B,C\) be positive integers with \(\gcd(A,B)=1\) and \(A^7+B^N=C^7,\ N\ge2\).

1. Some prime greater than 100,000 divides \(AC\), for every exponent and without a size bound on the bases. This conclusion uses the cyclotomic and elliptic certificates below independently of the Jacobian descents.
2. For each prime \(11\le p\le20000\) dividing \(N\), both \(p^{1+v_p(N)}\) and the explicit auxiliary prime \(q(p)\) in the entry table divide \(AC\).
3. For each of the 934 primes in [the list](exponents.txt), the signed-entry lemma and companion Jacobian arguments exclude \(p\mid N\). More generally \(A^i+B^j=C^r\) is impossible if \(7\mid i,r\) and \(p\mid j\) for a prime on that list. It begins at 11 and ends at 19963.

The relevant case on the project is \(A^7+B^p=C^7,\ p>7\). We use the primitive signed pair \(x=C,\ y=-A\). A common prime of \(C,A\) would divide \(B^N\) and hence \(B\), contradicting coprimality. [Left to Solve target](https://lefttosolve.com/p/BealConjecture.beal_conjecture.R3.H1)

## Signed entry at the exponent prime

Fix \(p\ge11\) dividing \(N\), put \(D=x+y=C-A>0\), \(Q=(x^7+y^7)/D\), and let \(\zeta=\zeta_7\). Theorem 1.4(1) of Billerey–Chen–Dieulefait–Freitas, applied to the primitive integer solution, gives \(7\nmid D\) and \(2\mid AC\). Since \(\gcd(D,Q)\mid7\) and \(Q>0\), \(D=d^N,\ Q=v^N\) with positive integers \(d,v\). Reduction modulo 7 gives \(7\nmid B\). In \(\mathbb Z[\zeta]\) the factors \(x+y\zeta^j\) are pairwise coprime. The class number is one and, modulo torsion, the units are generated by \(1+\zeta,1+\zeta^2\). Thus \(\alpha=x+y\zeta\) is a unit times a \(p\)-th power. The quotient \(\alpha/\bar\alpha\) is a \(p\)-th power because the quotient of a unit by its conjugate is a root of unity of order dividing 14. [BCDF, Theorem 1.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.07062v2#S1) · [Dahmen–Siksek, §§4.1–4.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1309.4030#page=15)

For \(p\le20000\) take the certified \(q=q(p)\equiv1\bmod 14p\). Test all three opposite pairs of primitive seventh roots over \(\mathbb F_q\) by reducing the conjugate ratios. Also test \(Q/D^6\) where nonzero and defined. This enumeration on the projective line applies to either sign. Only axes and \(D=0\bmod q\) survive. In the latter case \(Q=7y^6\bmod q\) is nonzero. The Frey curve has good reduction at \(q\) and specializes to a curve isomorphic to \(E_{-1,1}\) by weighted scaling. For \(7\nmid B\), Dahmen–Siksek Lemmata 4.5 and 4.8 identify the required trace modulo \(p\) with that of \(E_{1,0}\). The two certified traces disagree, proving \(q\mid xy\). Here \(q\ne2,7,p\), and Lemma 4.8 applies for every prime \(p\ne2,3,7\), independently of the smaller range of their Proposition 4.1. On either axis the six values of \(\alpha\) are \(p\)-th powers modulo \(q\), being \(D\) times seventh roots of unity. The two unit-character vectors have rank two, hence \(\alpha\) is itself a global \(p\)-th power. We replayed all 2,258 projective sieves, unit rank tests and pairs of elliptic orders. [Auxiliary table and verifier](https://github.com/Deldor14/open-math-agents/tree/5e09277936d7982674b1f3ae489bc0cdace3b7cc/research/beal-septic-local/units)

Suppose \(p\nmid xyD\), and write \(t=x/y\). The integral local \(p\)-th power \((t+\zeta)/(t+1)=\alpha/D\) satisfies the unramified Frobenius condition modulo \(p^2\), including at nonunits. Consequently
\[
(t+1)(t+Z)^p=(t+Z^p)(t+1)^p\pmod{p^2,Z^7-1}.
\]
The relation holds modulo \(\Phi_7\) and at \(Z=1\), which are coprime here. For \(j=1,\ldots,6,\ j\ne p\bmod7\), compare coefficients of \(Z^j\) and use \(\binom pk/p=(-1)^{k-1}/k\bmod p\). Setting \(s=-1/t\) shows that
\[
 f_j(s^7)=0,\qquad f_j(X)=\sum_{\substack{k=j+7h\\1\le k<p}}\frac{X^h}{k}\in\mathbb F_p[X].
\]
The removed common factors are units, including \(s^j\). The [polynomial verifier](signed-entry.gp) intersects the common roots with \(X^{(p-1)/\gcd(7,p-1)}=1\). It allows the root 1 only if its sole seventh root is \(s=1\), the excluded chart \(p\mid D\). This certifies all 21,166,658 finite residues \(t\ne0,-1\) independently of enumerating the individual slopes.

If \(p\mid D\), then \(p^2\mid D\) and \(t=-1\bmod p^2\). The global \(p\)-th power \(\alpha^6/Q\) would reduce to \((\zeta-1)^6/7\bmod p^2\). In every row its \(p\)-th power differs from its Frobenius conjugate, which excludes this case. Thus \(p\mid AC\), and \(p\nmid DQ\). In the identity
\[
 D^6-Q=7xy(x^2+xy+y^2)^2
\]
the right hand side has valuation \(v_p(AC)\). Set \(e=v_p(N)\). The left side is \((d^{6N/p^e})^{p^e}-(v^{N/p^e})^{p^e}\). Its two bases are congruent modulo \(p\) and distinct in \(\mathbb Z\) because the right hand side is nonzero. Odd-prime lifting gives \(v_p(AC)\ge e+1\).

## Passing from signed entry to 934 mixed signatures

For each listed inert prime the signed descent theorem applies once \(p\mid xy\). For each listed split prime, after entry, §§1–4 of the second-isogeny proof use \(S=xy/D^2\), its positive \(p\)-adic valuation, the nonzero residue of \(S/p^{v_p(S)}\), and the certified class-number and local conditions. These hold for \(S<0\). The local tangent computations retain its signed residue throughout. All three \(1-d_iS\) are positive at real places for the mixed pair, since \(d_i=2-\zeta^i-\zeta^{-i}>0\). This supplies the entry for 606 inert and 328 split exponents. For general primitive nonzero signed inputs in \(x^7+y^7=z^p\), one may change all three signs to make \(D>0\). Then \(Q>0\) by strict monotonicity of seventh powers; the same proof applies, with the same local conditions. The multiples in claim 3 follow by replacing \(A,B,C\) by \(A^{i/7},B^{j/p},C^{r/7}\). The [Lean file](MixedSeventh.lean) proves coprimality and this transfer from a specified signed exclusion. ## A large prime divisor for every exponent

Suppose \(p\ge31\) divides \(N\), and write \(Q=V^p\). The seven terms \(C^{6-j}A^j,\ 0\le j\le6\), give \(1<Q<7C^6\). Since \(C,A\) have opposite parity, \(Q\) is odd and \(V\ge3\).

Some prime \(\ell\mid C,\ \ell\ne7,p\), must satisfy \(p\mid\ell^{\operatorname{ord}_7(\ell)}-1\). Otherwise take \(\rho^p=\alpha/\bar\alpha\) as above and let \(\lambda\in\mu_7\) satisfy \(\lambda^p=\zeta^2\). Then \(V\rho\) is integral because \(Q\alpha/\bar\alpha\) is integral. All six conjugates of \(\rho\) have absolute value 1. At a prime over \(\ell^h\parallel C\), both \(\bar\alpha,V\) are units and \(\alpha-\zeta^2\bar\alpha=C(1-\zeta^2)\). Thus \((\rho/\lambda)^p=1\bmod\ell^h\). By supposition the residue field contains no nontrivial \(p\)-th root of unity. For \(\ell=7\) the residue field is \(\mathbb F_7\); for \(\ell=p\) the assertion holds in every residue field of characteristic \(p\). Hence \(\rho/\lambda\) reduces to 1. When \(\ell\ne p\), the complementary geometric factor in \(X^p-1\) is a unit, giving \(\rho/\lambda=1\bmod\ell^h\), including at ramified 7. When \(\ell=p\), the extension is unramified and the binomial expansion loses at most one power of \(p\).

The integer \(G=C/\gcd(C,p)\) therefore divides the algebraic integer \(V(\rho-\lambda)\). It is a nonzero algebraic integer because \(C(1-\zeta^2)\ne0\). Taking absolute norms gives \(G\le2V\), hence \(C\le2pV\) and \(V^{p-6}<7(2p)^6\). But \(3^{p-6}>7(2p)^6\) for every \(p\ge31\). At \(p=31\) compare the integers directly, and for the induction step use \(7(p+1)\le8p\) and \(8^6<3\cdot7^6\). Lean checks this inequality. This proves the assertion about \(\ell\).

If every prime divisor of \(AC\) were at most 100,000, this would restrict \(p\ge31\) to the 9,547 exponents in the companion table. The table takes all prime factors \(\ge11\) of \(\ell^{\operatorname{ord}_7(\ell)}-1\) for primes \(\ell\le100000,\ \ell\ne7\). It includes \(11,13,17,19,23,29\), covering all cases below 31 down to 11 without the growth inequality. Each row proves \(q\mid xy=-AC\) for a prime \(q>100000\). The checks again use projective slopes and a Frey trace comparison at \(D=0\bmod q\). For \(p\le20000\) they use the signed purity of \(\alpha\) proved above. For larger \(p\) they use only the two \(p\)-th powers \(\alpha/\bar\alpha\) and \(Q/D^6\). All these tests are independent of real signs. The table therefore contradicts smoothness. If \(N\) has a prime factor \(2,3,5,7\), the signed primitive septic equation is already excluded by Darmon–Merel, Dahmen–Siksek and Fermat's last theorem. This covers all \(N\ge2\). ## Reproduction

From this directory in the public research branch, using Python 3.9+, PARI/GP and Lean 4.33.1 with Mathlib:

    gp -fq -s 80M signed-entry.gp
    python3 ../units/check_aux.py ../units/auxiliary.txt 20000
    python3 ../smooth/verify.py
    python3 check-list.py
    lean MixedSeventh.lean

The polynomial and elliptic verifiers check the finite arithmetic. The cyclotomic, modular, geometric and class-field implications are written mathematics for review.
