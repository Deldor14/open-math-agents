# The exponent prime in a primitive sum of two seventh powers

Let $p\ge 11$ be prime. Suppose $a,b,c$ are positive integers, $\gcd(a,b)=1$, and $a^7+b^7=c^p$. Then **$p\nmid c$**. In particular $p$ divides neither $a+b$ nor its septic cofactor. At $p$ the Kraus Jacobian has good reduction. Its geometric $p$-rank is three if $p\equiv1\pmod7$ and zero otherwise.

This written argument uses the published residual comparison of Billerey, Chen, Dieulefait and Freitas (BCDF). The local argument at the residue characteristic uses finite flat group schemes. The two elementary end checks are also supplied in Lean.

### 1. Passing to a local field of small ramification

Write $K=\mathbb Q(\zeta_7)^+$, $J=J(a,b)$ for the genus-three Frey Jacobian, and $B$ for the Jacobian of $Y^2=X^7+1$. Fix a place of $K$ above $p$ and denote its completion by $k$. This is an unramified extension of $\mathbb Q_p$. For every coefficient prime of $K$ over $p$, the two-dimensional residual representations of $J$ and $B$ differ by a character of order at most two. Take $M/k$ to be the compositum of the local quadratic extensions killing these finitely many characters and the unramified quadratic extension. Since $p$ is odd, the unit square classes of $k$ have order two; adjoining a square root of a uniformizer accounts for the other generator. Thus $e(M/\mathbb Q_p)\le 2$, and $f(M/k)\le2$. [BCDF II, Theorem 1.4(2)](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.07062v2#S1)

After restriction to $G_M$, the six-dimensional mod-$p$ representations on $J[p]$ and $B[p]$ have the same semisimplification. To see this, decompose the rational $p$-adic Tate module into its real multiplication components, choose stable lattices in those components, and reduce. The semisimplification of the reduction is independent of stable lattice. Restriction of scalars and then extension to an algebraic closure of the residue coefficient field supply all three pairs of characters or two-dimensional constituents, counted with multiplicity. Equality descends to the common prime field by Brauer–Nesbitt. This argument uses only the rational real multiplication. [BCDF I, §2.2 and Theorem 3.11](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.15861v5#S2.SS2)

### 2. Consequence if $p$ divides $c$

Under this assumption $J$ has multiplicative reduction at the chosen place. In its toric uniformization the rational cocharacter space of the torus and the rational period lattice have dimension three. Each carries the action of $K$ and is one-dimensional over $K$. Galois over $k$ commutes with this action. Its finite unramified action on either rational lattice therefore takes values in the torsion of $K^\times$, which is $\{1,-1\}$. The unramified quadratic extension in $M$ splits both. Equivalently, apply the functorial period-lattice exact sequence of the totally toric uniformization over $M$. The six generic composition factors of $J[p]$ there are three copies of $\mathbb F_p$ and three copies of $\mathbb F_p(1)$. This describes semisimplification even when a Tate extension is nonsplit. [BCDF I, Proposition 5.14 and §7.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.15861v5#S5)

$B$ has good reduction at $p$, by its explicit smooth hyperelliptic model. Its $p$-torsion is finite flat over $\mathcal O_M$. A composition series of the generic fibre extends by schematic closures and finite flat quotients. Since $e(M/\mathbb Q_p)\le2<p-1$, Raynaud's full faithfulness identifies the finite flat models of the rank-one factors above uniquely as the constant group and $\mu_p$, respectively. Over the geometric special fibre their point groups have dimensions one and zero. Taking geometric points is exact in these finite group scheme sequences over an algebraically closed field. It follows that $B$ must be ordinary. Furthermore, over the residue field of $M$ the three composition factors on its geometric étale $p$-torsion are all trivial. [Raynaud, Corollaire 3.3.6](https://www.numdam.org/articles/10.24033/bsmf.1779/)

### 3. The reference curve

Put $n=(p-1)/2$. The Cartier–Manin matrix of $Y^2=X^7+1$ is, up to transpose convention, the matrix of coefficients of $X^{pi-j}$ in $(X^7+1)^n$, $1\le i,j\le3$. The $(i,j)$ entry is nonzero precisely when $j\equiv pi\pmod7$. When this congruence holds, the index $(pi-j)/7$ is between zero and $n$, and its binomial coefficient is nonzero modulo $p$. The matrix is a partial permutation with nonzero weights. Its stable rank counts the elements of $\{1,2,3\}$ whose whole multiplication-by-$p$ orbit stays in that set. Of the six units modulo 7, only the identity has any such orbit. This gives $p$-rank three for residue 1 and zero for residues 2 through 6. Thus ordinarity in §2 forces $p\equiv1\pmod7$, in which case $k=\mathbb Q_p$. [Achter and Howe, §§2–3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.10726)

Let $C_0$ denote the curve $Y^2=X^7+1$ and set $T=p+1-\#C_0(\mathbb F_p)$. The reduction of its Weil polynomial modulo $p$ has as its nonzero roots the eigenvalues of Frobenius on the geometric étale $p$-torsion of its Jacobian, with arithmetic Frobenius taken on Tate modules. This follows from the connected–étale decomposition of the $p$-divisible group, or equivalently the unit-root factor. Over the residue field of $M$ these three eigenvalues are all 1 by §2, counted with multiplicity. Since $f(M/\mathbb Q_p)$ is 1 or 2, already over $\mathbb F_p$ each is $+1$ or $-1$. Hence $T\equiv s\pmod p$ for some $s\in\{-3,-1,1,3\}$.

There are seven distinct roots of $X^7=-1$ in $\mathbb F_p$. They give seven points with $Y=0$. The point at infinity contributes one, and the other points occur in pairs. Consequently $T$ is even. By the Weil bound $T^2\le36p$. For $p\ge43$, $36p<(p-3)^2$, so $|T-s|<p$. The congruence would imply $T=s$, which contradicts parity. The only prime $11\le p<43$ equal to 1 modulo 7 is 29. Counting over $\mathbb F_{29}$ gives 24 points including infinity, so $T=6$, incompatible with the same four residues. This proves $p\nmid c$.

### 4. Consequence for good reduction

Now both Jacobians have good reduction at $p$. Their six-dimensional generic $p$-torsion representations over $M$ have the same composition factors by §1. A composition series on either side extends by schematic closure; corresponding generic simple factors have isomorphic finite flat models by the same full faithfulness theorem. Geometric special fibre point counts multiply in short exact sequences, so the two reductions have the same $p$-rank. Section 3 gives the stated rank for $J(a,b)$.

The argument leaves primitive solutions with $p\nmid c$ open. The Python verifier checks all six residue classes and counts the points for $p=29$ in two ways. The Lean file proves the parity obstruction for every $p\ge43$ and checks the exact count at 29 in the kernel. The arithmetic geometry in §§1–4 is a written proof for review.
