# A class-number criterion for septic powers at inert primes

**Written proof for review.** Let $p\ge11$ be prime, $K=\mathbb Q(\zeta_7)^+$, $M=K(\zeta_p)$. Suppose $p\equiv\pm2,\pm3\pmod7$ and $p\nmid h(M)$. Then a nontrivial primitive integer solution of $a^7+b^7=c^p$ must have $p\nmid ab$. This statement has no upper bound on $p$.

**Finite consequence.** For each of the 606 primes in `exponents.txt`, positive coprime integers $a,b$ cannot satisfy $a^7+b^7=C^N$ if $p\mid N$. The bases, their difference and $N$ are unrestricted apart from the stated conditions. The file gives the complete list passing the criterion among the 1,503 inert primes from 11 through 20,000. It begins $11,17,19,23,31,47,53$ and ends at $19913$. This combines the criterion with the previously certified necessity $p^2\mid ab$ for such a positive solution. [Entry certificates, LTS #150765](https://github.com/Deldor14/open-math-agents/tree/5e09277936d7982674b1f3ae489bc0cdace3b7cc/research/beal-septic-local/units)

We give the changes needed to the detailed [(7,7,11) proof, LTS #153217](https://github.com/Deldor14/open-math-agents/tree/b2d65981681cf44455709e953d70fe393a606e83/research/beal-septic-local/seven-eleven). The class-field argument below also supplies an alternative to the local unit determinants in its §2.

## 1. The initial point under the single condition that p divides ab

Put $z=\zeta_7$, $\theta=z+z^{-1}$, $D=a+b$, $Q=(a^7+b^7)/D$. Suppose $abc\ne0$ and $p\mid ab$. We have $7\nmid D$ by BCDF, so $\gcd(D,Q)=1$ and $D=v^p$, $Q=w^p$ with integer $v,w$. Also $p\nmid DQ$. The identity $D^6-Q=7ab(a^2+ab+b^2)^2$ gives $e=v_p(ab)\ge2$ since $p\mid(v^6)^p-w^p$ implies $p\mid v^6-w$ and hence $p^2\mid(v^6)^p-w^p$. Put $S=ab/D^2$. [BCDF II, Theorem 1.4(1)](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.07062v2#S1)

Write $d_1=2-\theta$, $d_2=4-\theta^2$, $F_i=1-d_iS$. We now show $F_i=G_i^p$ in $K$. In $\mathbb Q(z)$ the principal ideals of the seven factors $a+bz^j$, $0\le j\le6$, are pairwise coprime. A common prime would divide 7 and $a+b$, since $a,b$ are coprime. Their product is $(c)^p$. Each $(F_i)$ is therefore a $p$th power of a fractional ideal there, by taking a conjugate pair and dividing by $D^2$. The valuations in $K$ and in $M$ are also multiples of $p$, since the descent from $\mathbb Q(z)$ to $K$ has degree two. Locally at $p$, $F_i\in1+p^2\mathcal O_{K_p}$ is a $p$th power in the unramified field $K_p$ by the $p$-adic logarithm. The prime above $p$ is unique in $M$. The Kummer extension adjoining $F_i^{1/p}$ to $M$ is unramified everywhere, since away from $p$ the valuation is divisible by $p$ and a unit has separable $p$th-root reduction. By $p\nmid h(M)$ the extension is trivial. As $[M:K]=p-1$, taking norms and using Bezout gives a $p$th root in $K$. This step needs no auxiliary prime or bound on the exponent.

Put $u=(\theta+1)(\theta+2)$, $Y_0=2\theta+3$ and $W=2d_2-d_1$. The norms of $u,Y_0,W$ are $-1,-7,-49$. On $Y^2=1+4uX^p$, the point $P_0=(1,Y_0)$ and the point with $X=G_1/G_2^2$, $Y=(Y_0-d_2S)/(1-d_2S)$ are defined over $K$. This follows from $(Y_0-d_2S)^2=(1-d_2S)^2+4u(1-d_1S)$. Each $G_i\equiv1\pmod p$ since its $p$th power reduces to 1. With $h=e-1$ and $s=S/p^e\bmod p\in\mathbb F_p^\times$ we obtain $X-1\equiv p^h s W\pmod{p^{h+1}}$ by binomial expansion in $K_p$. All denominators here are local units.

## 2. Detection at the unique prime over p

Let $\xi=\zeta_p$, $\pi=\xi-1$, $k=M_{(\pi)}$ and $k_0=\mathbb F_{p^3}$. Here $p/\pi^{p-1}\equiv-1\pmod\pi$. For the Jacobian $J$ of $T(T-1)=uX^p$, $T=(1+Y)/2$, set $\tau(X,T)=(\xi X,T)$ and $\lambda=1-\tau_*$. Its kernel is generated by the rational class $H=[(0,0)-\infty]$ of order $p$. Indeed $\mathrm{div}(T)=p((0,0)-\infty)$, $H\ne0$ by positive genus, and the eigenvalues on degree-one cohomology are all nontrivial $p$th roots, once each. Thus $\deg\lambda=p$. The dual has the same kernel since $1-\tau_*^{-1}$ is an associate of $\lambda$. Evaluating $T$ on degree-zero divisors gives the injective Kummer map from $J(M)/\lambda J(M)$ to $M^\times/M^{\times p}$ and similarly locally, by the usual isogeny pairing. The point at infinity and the moving lemma allow representatives disjoint from the divisor of $T$. The valuation of every such global value is a multiple of $p$ at every finite prime. Indeed if $v(T)>0$ then $T-1$ is a unit, and if $v(T)<0$ then $v(T-1)=v(T)$; the equation and $v(u)=0$ give the assertion, also after taking norms. [Schaefer, explicit descent by functions](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.08325)

If the local class has a representative $1+\pi^p A$, its $p$th-root extension is unramified at $p$. The monic integral polynomial $((1+\pi V)^p-(1+\pi^p A))/\pi^p$ reduces to $V^p-V-\bar A$. This polynomial is separable, so all the roots lift in an unramified extension. At the other primes use the valuation condition above. Class field theory and $p\nmid h(M)$ therefore make the global class trivial. In particular whenever a global point has a prescribed local $\lambda$-lift, this lift itself belongs to $J(M)$, since some global lift exists and the entire kernel is rational. This is the point where having a single place above $p$ matters.

## 3. Formal division and the trace

Write $g=(p-1)/2$. The finite flat double-cover model has the infinity chart $v^2-z^{g+1}v=uz$ with $z=X^{-1}$ and $v=Tz^{g+1}$. Its special fibre is geometrically integral because $T(T-1)/\bar u$ has a simple zero in $\overline k_0(T)$. Infinity is smooth. The formal Picard group at the identity is smooth of dimension $g$ by the deformation theory of line bundles on a curve. Its cotangent basis is $X^{j-1}dX/Y$, $1\le j\le g$, by adjunction including at infinity. Formal coordinates are given by sums of displacements of $g$ smooth points with distinct abscissas $r_i$ and nonzero $T_i,Y_i$. Indeed the differential has invertible Vandermonde matrix $(r_i^{j-1}/Y_i)$. For the requisite sections, set $d=\bar u^{1/p}$ and choose nonzero $Z_i\ne\pm1$ with distinct squares in $k_0$; use $r_i=(Z_i^2-1)/(4d)$, $Y_i=Z_i^p$ and lift by Hensel. There are more than $g$ choices of square. [Conrad, Picard functors for curves](https://math.stanford.edu/~conrad/248BPage/handouts/pic.pdf)

In these coordinates let $F^m$ have all displacements in $\pi^m\mathcal O_k$. The action of $\tau$ is the identity on the special fibre, so every coefficient of the formal map $\lambda$ is divisible by $\pi$. The linear part after division has eigenvalues $-j$ modulo $\pi$ in tangent coordinates paired with the indicated differentials. This follows from $\tau^*\omega_j=\xi^j\omega_j$. The formal inverse function theorem now gives a bijection $\lambda:F^m\to F^{m+1}$ for $m\ge1$. The class $[P-P_0]$ has depth $(p-1)h$ and its leading tangent components are all a common scalar in $\mathbb F_p^\times$ times $W/Y_0$. Divide $n=(p-1)h-1$ times to obtain $B\in F^1$. Each local lift is global by §2. Since $(-j)^{-n}=-j$ in $\mathbb F_p$, the leading vector of $B$ at depth one has components $c_0j W/Y_0$ for one $c_0\in\mathbb F_p^\times$.

Write the section displacements of $B$ as $\pi b_i\bmod\pi^2$. In what follows abscissas, slopes, coefficients and tangents are in $k_0$. Its Kummer value is $1+\pi^p L\bmod\pi^{p+1}$, where
$$ L=\sum_i\kappa_i(b_i^p-r_i^{p-1}b_i),\qquad \kappa_i=u/(Y_iT_i). $$
Indeed the only contributing terms in $(r_i+\pi b_i)^p-r_i^p$ are the linear and final ones. By §2 the global class is trivial, so $L$ must have absolute trace zero, by expanding $(1+\pi b)^p$.

Set $Z_i=Y_i^{1/p}$ and $H(X)=[1-(1+4dX)^g]/(2X)$, a polynomial of degree $g-1$. We have $Z_i^2=1+4d r_i$, $\kappa_i^{1/p}=2d/(Z_i(Z_i+1))$, and $r_i\kappa_i^{1/p}=(Z_i-1)/(2Z_i)$. Multiplying the latter and its $p$th power by $Y_i$ gives $Y_i(\kappa_i^{1/p}-r_i^{p-1}\kappa_i)=H(r_i)$. Frobenius invariance of trace changes $\operatorname{Tr} L$ to the trace of $\sum_i H(r_i)b_i/Y_i$. By the tangent components above, evaluation on any polynomial $f$ of degree at most $g-1$ gives $c_0 W(f(1)+f'(1))/Y_0$. In characteristic $p$, $(XH)'=d(1+4dX)^{g-1}$. Thus, with $Z_0=Y_0^{1/p}$, the required trace is
$$ c_0\operatorname{Tr}(W d/Z_0^3)=c_0\operatorname{Tr}(W^p u/Y_0^3). $$
The Frobenius at $p$ sends $\theta$ to $\theta^2-2$ for $p\equiv\pm2\bmod7$ and to $1-\theta-\theta^2$ for $p\equiv\pm3\bmod7$. The two rational traces of $\sigma(W)u/Y_0^3$ are **3 and 4**, respectively. Indeed after multiplying by 7 their coordinate triples in the basis $1,\theta,\theta^2$ are $(-4,-3,6)$ and $(-1,-6,5)$, and the basis has traces $3,-1,5$. Reduction is valid since $p\ne7$. In either case the result contradicts trace zero. This proves the unbounded criterion.

## 4. The finite class-number checks

It suffices to test the minus class number. For completeness, if the minus $p$-class group of $M$ is trivial but its $p$-class group is nontrivial, complex conjugation acts trivially on the latter. Choose a degree-$p$ quotient of the Hilbert class field. Complex conjugation fixes this extension and acts trivially on its relative Galois group. The Kummer pairing then puts its radical in the minus part of $M^\times/M^{\times p}$, since conjugation inverts $\mu_p$. All valuations are multiples of $p$. Sending the radical to the ideal class of its $p$th-root ideal gives a minus class in $\mathrm{Cl}(M)[p]$, hence zero. The radical is therefore represented by a global unit. For any unit $e$ the ratio $e/\bar e$ is a root of unity by Kronecker. As 2 is invertible modulo $p$, the minus part of the unit quotient is generated by $\zeta_p$. Its nonzero classes generate $M(\zeta_{p^2})$, which is ramified at $p$. This contradicts the choice of an unramified extension.

The abelian relative class-number formula gives $h^-=Q_M w_M\prod_{\chi\ {\rm odd}}(-B_{1,\chi}/2)$, using primitive characters. Here $Q_M$ is 1 or 2 and $v_p(w_M)=1$. For an odd character of conductor $f$, $B_{1,\chi}=\sum_{a=1}^f a\chi(a)/f$. Write $\omega$ for the Teichmüller character mod $p$ and $\eta$ for a nontrivial cubic character mod 7. The odd characters are $\omega^k\eta^j$ with $k$ odd modulo $p-1$ and $j=0,1,2$. For $j=0,k=-1$, the numerator is $-1\bmod p$ and cancels the valuation of $w_M$. Every other factor is integral at $p$ by the formulas below. Thus test each for a nonzero residue. [Relative class-number formula, Proposition A.2](https://warwick.ac.uk/fac/sci/maths/people/staff/cwilliams/lecturenotes/lecture_notes_part_i.pdf)

In `bernoulli.gp` take a primitive root $g$ and $a_i=g^i\bmod p$ in $[1,p-1]$. Put $t_i=a_i^p\bmod p^2$. For $j=0,k\ne-1$, $\sum_i t_i^{k+1}=0\bmod p^2$ and the residues are the evaluations of $\sum_i((a_i-t_i)/p)X^i$ at $X=g^k$. For the cubic twist, group the primitive sum by $a_i$: $\sum_{m=0}^6\eta(a_i+mp)=0$, leaving the coefficient $\sum_{m=0}^6 m\eta(a_i+mp)/7$. For the nonvanishing test omit the unit factor $1/7$. The norm of the resulting polynomial $A+\eta(3)B$ is $A^2-AB+B^2$. Taking gcds with $X^{(p-1)/2}+1$ checks every odd character, omitting $g^{-1}$ only in the untwisted test. Everything is computed over $\mathbb F_p$.

In `character-values.gp` the untwisted test instead uses $\lfloor ga_i/p\rfloor$. Indeed multiplying the primitive residue representatives by $g$ and reducing modulo $p$ gives $(g\chi(g)-1)B_{1,\chi}=\chi(g)\sum_a\lfloor ga/p\rfloor\chi(a)$. The multiplier is nonzero modulo $p$ except for the omitted character. The cubic sums are independently accumulated over integers $1\le a<7p$ coprime to $7p$. This time the program evaluates the characters individually by a chirp convolution and counts the zero values. Both programs return the same two zero counts for each of the 1,503 primes. The twisted column counts an odd exponent once when either cubic character vanishes.

A third check uses the unconditional Aoki–Fukuda algorithm in PARI over the fixed field of the automorphism $-1$ modulo 7 and $1$ modulo $p$. The default flag gives the exact minus order and a rigorous upper bound for the plus order. All 441 inert primes through 5,000 agree with the two character tests on triviality. The finite-field tangent verifier separately solves the full linear system at every inert prime through 100. The Lean file checks the characteristic-zero polynomial identities and trace numerators. The geometric and class-field steps above remain written mathematics for review. [PARI, subcyclopclgp](https://pari.math.u-bordeaux.fr/dochtml/html-stable/General_number_fields.html#subcyclopclgp)
