# How much can a universal rounding rule transfer between two interval lengths?

Let $R(n)=r_3(n)$ be the largest size of a subset of $[n]=\{0,\ldots,n-1\}$ with no nonconstant three-term arithmetic progression. One approach to growth of $R$ is to dilate by a factor $\lambda$ while choosing which output sites to keep and which input site each represents. This note computes the exact asymptotic occupancy possible when one rule must work for every free input and the distance to a linear map is bounded. It also gives a finite inequality for $R$ and a quadratic obstruction above the computed occupancy.

Fix $1\le\lambda\le4$ and put $\alpha=1/\lambda$. A rule consists of $S_N\subseteq[N]$ and an integer label $f_N(y)$ at each $y\in S_N$, with
$$ |f_N(y)-\alpha y-b_N|\le C $$
for a fixed finite $C$ and an arbitrary intercept $b_N$. A free input $A\subseteq\mathbb Z$ selects the output points $y\in S_N$ for which $f_N(y)\in A$. Call the rule universal if this output is always free. Define
$$
D(\alpha)=
\begin{cases}
(p+1)/(2p), & \alpha=q/p\text{ in lowest terms and }p\text{ odd},\\
1/2, & \alpha\text{ irrational or its reduced denominator is even}.
\end{cases}
$$

**Density theorem.** Every family of universal rules has $\limsup |S_N|/N\le D(\alpha)$. This value is attained with $C=1/2$ by a single rule on all the integers. In particular the optimal output-site density at dilation $3/2$ is $2/3$, at $4/3$ it is $1/2$, and at an irrational dilation it is $1/2$. Arbitrarily long periods or nonperiodic labels are allowed.

For an interval of $N$ output sites the labels span an interval of length at most $\alpha N+O_C(1)$. The normalized mean number of copies per potential input site, using an input window of that length, consequently has sharp upper limit $\lambda D(\alpha)$. For every $1<\lambda<2$ this is at most one. For an odd numerator $p$ in $\lambda=p/q<2$, use $p+1\le2q$. This restricts the gain available from unconditional averaging of such rules. One can still use the locations of points in a particular extremizer or a different construction. Indeed the [separated-interval argument](RothGrowth.md) proves proportional growth of the actual Roth number at some dilations below two.

**Robustness.** For every $\delta>0$ and fixed $C,\alpha$, there exist $c>0$ and $N_0$ such that if $N\ge N_0$ and $|S_N|\ge(D(\alpha)+\delta)N$, at least $cN^2$ increasing output progressions have three distinct input labels forming a progression-free set. Each of these three-point inputs witnesses failure of universality.

## Attainment and the finite transfer

For a real shift $b$, accept the integers $y$ with $\{\alpha y+b\}\in[0,1/2)$ and assign $f(y)=\lfloor\alpha y+b\rfloor$. On an output progression the second difference of the fractional parts is an integer strictly between $-1$ and $1$, hence is zero. The three integer labels form a progression. They cannot all coincide: one label occupies an interval of length $\lambda/2\le2$, half open, which contains at most two integers. For rational $\alpha=q/p$ take $b=0$. Exactly $\lceil p/2\rceil$ of the $p$ residues are accepted. For irrational $\alpha$ the density is $1/2$ by equidistribution.

For every integer $n\ge1$ this gives the genuine finite inequality
$$
R\!\left(\left\lceil\lambda(n-1/2)\right\rceil\right)
\ \ge\ \left\lceil\lambda D(\alpha) R(n)\right\rceil. \tag{1}
$$
For rational slope translate an optimal input by $k=0,\ldots,q-1$. The multiplicities of its labels are $q$-periodic and their sum in one period is $\lceil p/2\rceil$. For irrational slope average $b$ over $[0,\alpha)$: for each input $x$, the intervals traced by $\alpha y+b$ as $b$ varies partition the line, so its mean multiplicity is $1/(2\alpha)$. In both cases all selected sites lie in a half-open interval of length $\lambda(n-1/2)$ and translate into the interval on the left of (1).

## Reflection and a finite rigidity lemma

For a universal rule, an output progression $y-d,y,y+d$ with $d>2C/\alpha$ must have midpoint-linear labels. Indeed, the labels are strictly increasing, and if they failed the midpoint equation the three labels themselves would be a free input. The same midpoint equation holds for the residual $h(y)=f(y)-\alpha y$.

First consider a rational slope and a periodic template of period $P$, meaning that $S$ is periodic and $f(y+P)=f(y)+\alpha P$. Assume $S$ is nonempty, so $p\mid P$. Every modular midpoint equation in $S\bmod P$ gives one for $h$: realize the modular triple by an integer progression of arbitrarily large common difference in the same residue classes. At a maximum point $v$ of $h$, every point of $S\cap(2v-S)$ has maximal residual. A fixed residual occupies one residue modulo $p$. Reflection gives $2|S|-P\le P/p$, counting one period. This proves the odd-denominator bound.

For even denominators we use a stronger fact. **A real midpoint-linear function on more than half of a cyclic group of order $2^k$ is constant on its domain.** For order one this is immediate. Otherwise one parity class has more than half its sites occupied, and the inductive hypothesis makes its labels constant. Reflection about any occupied center preserves that parity class. Two subsets each occupying more than half of it intersect, so the center has two endpoints from the constant set and has their value. The [Lean file](RothRounding.lean) proves this induction on integer representatives, including the prescribed-sum pair and the exact parity count.

Write $P=2^a m$ with $m$ odd. If more than half of a period were occupied, some progression of step $m$ modulo $P$ would be more than half occupied. On it the lemma makes $h$ constant. A constant residual forces the output coordinate into one residue modulo $p$, hence into one parity class. The chosen progression has even length and odd step; at most half its sites have that parity. This proves the exact periodic result.

## Limits of finite interval rules

Subtract an integer from the labels so the intercept belongs to $[0,1)$. Encode each finite rule by a two-sided sequence of residuals in a fixed compact interval, with a separate isolated symbol for unoccupied sites, extending by that symbol outside $[N]$. Include a circle coordinate recording the phase $\alpha y\bmod1$ of the origin, which advances by $\alpha$ under a shift. At rational slope this is a finite cyclic coordinate. Take the empirical law after recentering at a uniform point of $[N]$. Any weak limit is shift-invariant by the boundary estimate $2/N$, and has occupancy equal to the limiting density along the chosen subsequence. At each occupied point $j$ a residual $h_j$ satisfies $h_j+\alpha j+\theta\in\mathbb Z$, with $\theta$ the origin phase. Every sufficiently long occupied progression satisfies the residual midpoint equation. Both properties survive because they are closed conditions on each finite pattern.

If the limiting density is $d$, some realization has upper symmetric density at least $d$. This follows from reverse Fatou applied to its occupancies in $[-L,L]$, whose expectations are all $d$. Fix a point $v$ in this realization of residual within $\epsilon$ of the supremum. Along a subsequence of these symmetric intervals, reflecting the occupied set about $v$ yields an overlap proportion at least $2d-1-o(1)$; shifting the interval by $2v$ costs $O(|v|)$ boundary sites. Apart from $O_{C,\alpha}(1)$ short progressions, both residuals of every reflected pair are within $2\epsilon$ of the supremum. Their phases lie in an arc of length $2\epsilon$. For irrational $\alpha$ its upper frequency in all integers is $2\epsilon$, giving $d\le1/2$ by passage to the limit. At rational slope choose $2\epsilon<1/p$. The frequency is at most $1/p$, giving the odd-denominator result for arbitrary finite templates.

## Even denominators without periodicity

Suppose $\alpha=q/p$ with $p$ even, and suppose a sequence of exact rules has limiting density greater than $1/2$. In the preceding stationary limit choose an ergodic component with occupancy greater than $1/2$. Record the finite integer label $\ell=p h$ on occupied points. It has parity fixed by the phase of the site, since $q$ is odd. In particular one fixed label can occur on only one of the two parities. The system includes the parity factor, the rotation interchanging the two parities.

Let $G$ be the compact metrizable abelian group of the Kronecker factor, with Haar measure and its ergodic rotation. For each integer label $i$ let $g_i$ be the conditional expectation on this factor of the indicator of that label. The parity factor is included because its character is an eigenfunction. For $i+k\ne2j$ there are no occurrences of those labels on sufficiently long progressions. Furstenberg's characteristic-factor theorem for three-term progressions therefore gives
$$
\int_{G^2} g_i(x-r)g_j(x)g_k(x+r)\,dx\,dr=0.
$$
On the rotation this formula follows by averaging its dense cyclic translation and approximating bounded functions in $L^1$ by bounded continuous functions. [Furstenberg, ergodic proof of Szemerédi's theorem](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~gasarch/TOPICS/vdw/furstenbergsz.pdf) · [Kra, characteristic factors for progression averages](https://sites.math.northwestern.edu/~kra/papers/montreal.pdf)

Write $E_i=\{g_i>0\}$. After discarding a Haar-null set, these supports contain **no** pointwise progression with the forbidden labels, even for $r=0$. Here is a justification for this passage before restricting to a possibly Haar-null subgroup. Take symmetric absolutely continuous probability kernels $\nu_m$ supported in shrinking neighborhoods of zero. Their convolutions and their convolution squares approximate each $1_{E_i}$ in $L^1$. Pass to a common subsequence converging almost everywhere for both kernels and all the finitely many sets. At a purported surviving triple perturb the center by $u$ and the difference by $v$, independently with law $\nu_m$. The center marginal uses $\nu_m$, and each endpoint marginal uses $\nu_m*\nu_m$. With probability tending to one all three perturbed points still lie in the respective supports. This contradicts the zero integral because the joint law of perturbed center and difference is absolutely continuous on $G^2$. Remove also the null exceptions to the parity constraints. At $r=0$ the same argument excludes membership in two distinct label supports. Thus the union $E$ has a single-valued midpoint-linear label; its measure is at least the occupancy since $\sum_i g_i\le1$ and each $g_i$ is supported on $E_i$ up to null sets.

There is a continuous homomorphism $\iota:\mathbb Z_2\longrightarrow G$ on which the parity character is nontrivial, where $\mathbb Z_2$ is the additive group of 2-adic integers. To see this, in the countable discrete character group $\widehat G$ send the parity character to $1/2$ in the group of dyadic rationals modulo one. Extend this homomorphism one generator at a time. If a least positive multiple of the new generator already belongs to the previous subgroup, choose its value by dividing the assigned multiple in the divisible group of dyadic rationals modulo one. If no such multiple exists, choose zero. For $t\in\mathbb Z_2$, evaluate a dyadic fraction $a/2^k$ at $t$ as $a(t\bmod 2^k)/2^k$ modulo one. Composing gives a character of $\widehat G$, hence an element $\iota(t)$ of $G$ by Pontryagin duality. Continuity follows character by character and parity on $\iota(t)$ is $t\bmod2$.

For Haar-uniform $x\in G$, the expected measure of $\{t:x+\iota(t)\in E\}$ is $|E|>1/2$ by Fubini. Fix an $x$ for which the measure exceeds $1/2$. On this set of 2-adic integers we have a finite-valued measurable label satisfying the midpoint equation pointwise. Such a label is constant on any domain of measure greater than $1/2$. Indeed follow the nested dyadic residue classes of a random point, and stop if the conditional occupancy first falls to $1/2$ or lower. If the initial occupancy is $d>1/2$, the probability of never stopping is at least $2d-1$: the disjoint stopped balls have occupancy at most $1/2$, and the remaining points have occupancy at most one. Almost every point of the surviving set is an occupied point: dyadic conditional expectations converge to the indicator and stay above $1/2$ on this set. Choose a surviving density point of one label, using the same differentiation for the finite label partition. Some small ball on its path has that label on more than half of its sites in Haar measure. Reflection about any occupied point in its parent preserves this child ball, so there is a pair of these equal labels centered at that point. The whole occupied parent consequently has the same label. Its occupancy exceeds $1/2$ along the chosen path. Induct back up to the whole group. This contradicts parity, since each label is allowed on just one half of $\mathbb Z_2$. The proof covers finite or infinite 2-primary images of $\iota$.

## A positive proportion of offending triples above the threshold

Suppose along a sequence of lengths that the occupancy exceeds $D(\alpha)+\delta$ and that only $o(N^2)$ increasing progressions have distinct labels forming a free set. The $O_{C,\alpha}(N)$ progressions of short step may be included in the error. Among all the other progressions the labels are ordered strictly and the only obstruction is a nonzero integer second difference. Partition the bounded residual range into finitely many bins of width at most $1/10$. For any ordered triple of bins possible residual second differences lie in an interval of width at most $4/10$. On an output progression they are integers. A bin triple allowing a nonzero integer therefore cannot allow zero.

For each such bin triple apply the colored arithmetic removal lemma for the equation $x+z=2y$ in a prime field of order between $2N$ and $4N$. The equation has no extra modular solutions inside $[N]$. Reversed orientations multiply the count by at most two. Deleting $o(N)$ sites from each of finitely many bin sets destroys all these configurations. The remaining sites have exact midpoint-linear labels on every progression and density still above the ceiling. The upper proofs only used midpoint linearity on long progressions, so the contradiction applies even if a short progression originally had a repeated label. This proves the claimed quadratic bound without an effective constant. [Král', Serra and Vena, removal lemma over finite fields](https://arxiv.org/abs/0809.1846)
