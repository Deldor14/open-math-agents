# Progression-free sets meeting every long odd-step progression

For $k\ge3$, let $r_k(n)$ be the maximum number of integers in $[0,n)$ avoiding nonconstant $k$-term arithmetic progressions, and put $d_k(n)=\log(n/r_k(n))$. All logarithms are natural. Define $L_{k,B}(n)$ for a fixed integer $B\ge2$ as the least positive $L$ for which there exists a $k$-term-free set in $[0,n)$ meeting every $L$-term arithmetic progression of positive step coprime to $B$. For $B=2$ this means that the complement contains no $L$-term progression of odd step. The definition uses only the two colors and their prohibited progressions.

For every fixed $B\ge2$, as $n\to\infty$,
$$d_3(n)-o(1)\ \le\ \log L_{3,B}(n)\ \le\ d_3(n)+4\sqrt{d_3(n)}+O_B(\log\log n).\tag{1}$$
There is a stronger simultaneous counting statement. For $k=3$ write $d=d_3(n)$. There is a power $b$ of $B$ in the upper size bound in (1), a three-term-free $A\subset[0,n)$, and a density $\delta$ for which
$$|A|=(1+o(1))n\delta,\qquad
\log(n/|A|)\le d+4\sqrt d+O_B(1).\tag{2}$$
Every progression of $b$ terms and positive step coprime to $B$ contains $(1+O(1/\log n))\delta b$ elements of this same $A$, uniformly in step and initial point. For every length $s\ge b$, the count in each such progression is $\delta s(1+O(1/\log n+b/s))$. Alternatively one may choose $b$ prime in the same bounds, in which case every step apart from multiples of this prime is covered. In particular, the odd-step question determines the original logarithmic density deficit up to relative error tending to zero. The asymptotic equivalent of $r_3(n)$ is still unknown. [Erdős 142](https://www.erdosproblems.com/latex/142)

The dyadic extension covers all steps except the multiples of a chosen, growing power of two and gives the corresponding mass and window bounds. [Beyond odd steps](RothDyadicWindows.md)

## The finite counting theorem

Choose positive integers $b,T$ with $n\le bT$, a tag set $U\subset[0,\lceil b/2\rceil)$ of size $s$, and a height set $V\subset[0,T)$ of size $m$. Both sets must avoid nonconstant $k$-term progressions. Let $\mathcal F$ be any finite family of $b$-term progressions in $[0,n)$ with step coprime to $b$. Let $H$ be any set of forbidden intersection cardinalities. The following integer test suffices:
$$|\mathcal F|\sum_{j\in H\cap[0,s]}{s\choose j}m^j(2T-m)^{s-j}<(2T)^s.\tag{3}$$
There exists a $k$-term-free $A\subset[0,n)$ with $|A\cap P|\notin H$ for every $P\in\mathcal F$. Lean verifies this statement against the progression-free predicate in the upstream Erdős file, for every $k\ge2$. Composite moduli are allowed. [Finite theorem](RothPrimeTransversals.lean) · [Exact count of failed assignments](RothRowSelection.lean) · [Integer rows and invertible steps](RothPrimeRows.lean)

Choose a shift $s_u$ independently and uniformly in $[0,2T)$ for every tag $u\in U$. Keep $z<n$ precisely when $u=z\bmod b$ is a tag and $\lfloor z/b\rfloor+s_u\in T+V$. If the kept points contained a $k$-term progression, its successive triples would satisfy midpoint equations. Reducing each equation modulo $b$ gives equality of integer tag sums since all such sums are less than $b$. The $k$ tags would therefore form a progression in $U$, allowing either orientation or zero step. They must all coincide. The heights on this one row then give an ordinary nonconstant progression in a translate of $V$, which is impossible.

Each $P$ visits every residue exactly once. At its unique height $h$ above any tag $u$, the successful shifts are $T+v-h$ for $v\in V$. All $m$ distinct choices lie in $[0,2T)$. Thus $|A\cap P|$ has the exact binomial law with $s$ trials and success probability $m/(2T)$. Summing the probabilities of forbidden counts over $P$ proves (3). Different tests can be arbitrarily dependent. The proof also works for any supplied family of sets meeting every residue exactly once.

A deterministic version chooses the shifts in increasing tag order. Given fixed earlier shifts, compute for each possible next shift the number of completions that fail each test, and sum over the tests. The sum of these integers over the $2T$ choices is the previous total. Choosing a minimum maintains the strict inequality with the number of remaining assignments. At the end the integer number of failed tests is zero. Each conditional count is the same binomial sum with the known hits added and the remaining number of rows as the trial count.

## Choice of parameters

Write $\alpha=r_k(n)/n$, $d=d_k(n)$ and $\epsilon=1/\log(2n)$. Choose $b$ as specified below, $T=\lceil n/b\rceil$, and extremal $V$ and $U$ on $T$ and $\lceil b/2\rceil$ sites respectively. We will have $b=n^{o(1)}$. By partitioning $[0,n)$ into intervals of length at most $T$, $r_k(n)\le\lceil n/T\rceil m$, so $m/(2T)\ge\alpha/4$ when $T\le n$. By Behrend and $r_k\ge r_3$,
$$s\ge(b/2)\exp(-4\sqrt{\log b}),\quad
\mu:=sm/(2T)\ge \alpha b\exp(-4\sqrt{\log b})/8.$$
Define $h=d+\log(120\epsilon^{-2}\log(2n))$. Take the least power of $B$ at least $\exp((2+\sqrt{h+4})^2)$, or a prime between the ceiling of that quantity and twice its ceiling by Bertrand's theorem. The function $x-4\sqrt x$ increases for $x>4$, and at $x=(2+\sqrt{h+4})^2$ it equals $h$. Hence
$$\log b\le h+4\sqrt{h+4}+8+O_B(1),\qquad \mu\ge15\epsilon^{-2}\log(2n).\tag{4}$$
Behrend also gives $d\le4\sqrt{\log n}$, which verifies $b=n^{o(1)}$ for every fixed $k$. The ordinary multiplicative Chernoff bound for a binomial variable puts the probability of a deviation exceeding $\epsilon\mu$ at most $2\exp(-\epsilon^2\mu/3)$. There are at most $n^2$ progressions to test, so (3) applies. For any $s'\ge b$, take $\lfloor s'/b\rfloor$ disjoint subprogressions of $b$ consecutive terms and cover the remainder by the last $b$ terms. This bounds the count between $(1-\epsilon)\mu\lfloor s'/b\rfloor$ and $(1+\epsilon)\mu\lceil s'/b\rceil$. Applying it to $[0,n)$ gives (2) with $\delta=\mu/b$. In general $\log(1/\delta)\le d+4\sqrt{\log b}+\log 8$.

The lower bound holds for every $k$: disjoint consecutive $L$-point intervals force $\lfloor n/L\rfloor\le r_k(n)$, so $L>n/(r_k(n)+1)$. For $k=3$, Raghavan's lower bound on $d$ implies $\log\log n=o(\sqrt d)$. Thus $h=d+O(\log\log n)$ and $\sqrt{\log b}=\sqrt d+O_B(1)$, which proves (1) and (2). For arbitrary fixed $k\ge3$ the unconditional bounds preceding this simplification still apply. In particular the liminf and limsup of $\log(1+\log L_{k,B}(n))/\log\log n$ equal those of $\log(1+d_k(n))/\log\log n$ respectively: the upper additive error in $\log L$ is $O_B(\log\log n+\sqrt{d+\log\log n})$, and taking the outer logarithm changes the normalized quantities by $o(1)$. [Behrend bound in mathlib](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Combinatorics/Additive/AP/Three/Behrend.html) · [Raghavan, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27045v3)

The respective numbers of starting positions are $1281,771,261$. In (3) forbidding counts below 3 or above 21 gives a ratio of the left to the right side below $0.861$. ## Relation to earlier work

Nathanson studied sets with bounded consecutive gaps and sequences taking one point in each fixed-width interval. The parameter above imposes a condition on the complement along every progression with an allowed step and bounds the counts there simultaneously. Unrestricted asymmetric van der Waerden numbers require the complement to avoid progressions of every step. Steps sharing a factor with the chosen modulus are outside the finite theorem; for example an unused tag yields an empty residue class. [Nathanson (1980)](https://doi.org/10.4153/CMB-1980-074-x) · [Green on the unrestricted problem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.01543)

On the Left to Solve board, konradsiebor used traversal of residue classes to determine exact maxima on short intervals, including a common-divisor obstruction at a specific endpoint. The q=4 work concerns the number of deletions in that short-interval problem. The independent choice of an entire row per tag in (3) also enforces avoidance of a separately prescribed length $k$.
