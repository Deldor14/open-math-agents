# Extending prescribed progression-free subsets to sets meeting every long progression

For an integer N let [N] mean the integers from 0 through N−1. A progression always has positive step. Write r_k(N) for the largest size of a subset of [N] containing no k-term arithmetic progression. Call a set L-hitting if it meets every L-term progression in [N], with no restriction on the step.

**Partition theorem.** For every 0 < ε ≤ 1 and all sufficiently large N depending only on ε, there is a partition of [N] into at most exp(C/ε) parts. Each part P has a fixed set B disjoint from P such that

* B is 3-free and meets every progression of length ⌈N^ε⌉.
* For **every** k ≥ 3 and every k-free T ⊆ P, the set T ∪ B is k-free.

The partition and the sets B may be chosen before T, k, or any site weights are supplied. C is absolute.

In particular, given any k-free S ⊆ [N] and nonnegative site weights, some k-free set meeting every such progression retains at least exp(−C/ε) of the weight of S on its original sites. For unit weights and an extremal input, the constrained maximum lies between exp(−C/ε) r_k(N) and r_k(N). This gives a bounded additive change in the logarithmic density deficit for fixed ε. It leaves the asymptotic formula in Erdős 142 open.

## A finite-parameter statement

Suppose H ⊆ [N] is 3-free and meets every ℓ-term progression, ℓ ≥ 2. For each sufficiently large integer r put

$$
s=\lceil r/1000\rceil,\qquad
\eta=\tfrac12 e^{-101r}(4N)^{-1/s},\qquad
M=\lceil 400^r/\eta\rceil,\qquad
L=2M+1+(\ell-1)(400\ell)^r.
$$

There is a partition as above into at most 50^r parts with L in place of ⌈N^ε⌉. An assertion about L-term progressions is vacuous if L > N. The estimates used below give nonvacuous lengths for large N.

The external inputs are Haitao Cao's finite Fourier set and center-selection lemmas, followed at the end by his two-color van der Waerden bound. We give the variable-length orbit argument because uniformity in N at fixed r matters here. [Cao, Theorem 1.1 and Lemmas 2.1 and 2.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.31798v1)

### A rotation that enters an auxiliary box for every direction

Use the torus of dimension r with its maximum coordinate distance to zero. Write Q_t for the open box of radius t about zero. From Cao's two lemmas take the following data.

* A continuous nonnegative function Ψ supported on the closure of Q_(1/400), positive precisely inside it. Its Fourier coefficients are nonnegative, sum to one, and have constant term 400^(−r). A frequency set F containing zero has size at most exp(100r) and omitted coefficient sum less than 400^(−r)/100.
* Centers z_i such that z_i−2z_j+z_h lies outside Q_(1/25) whenever i,j,h are not all equal. Given any rational space V generated by elements of F of dimension less than r/1000 and any phase y, at least two distinct centers have corrections u_i in Q_(1/400) satisfying ξ·(y−z_i−u_i)=0 in the circle for every integer ξ in V. Cao gives exponentially many choices. Fix one center z_0 for the prescribed sites.

Take θ Haar-uniform on the torus. For any fixed 1 ≤ q ≤ N and s independent frequency vectors, their evaluations on qθ are jointly Haar on the s-torus. Indeed the integer matrix of the map has full row rank over the reals, hence induces a surjective homomorphism on the tori. The probability that all s evaluations are within η of zero is (2η)^s. A union bound over q and ordered frequency tuples gives failure probability at most N (exp(100r) 2η)^s = exp(−rs)/4. Fix a successful θ. For each q the space V spanned by frequencies ξ in F with ||ξ·qθ|| ≤ η has dimension at most s−1 < r/1000.

For an arbitrary starting phase γ let y=γ+M qθ. Choose a corrected center z_i with i≠0 for y and this V. Average Ψ(y−z_i−u_i+m qθ) against the probability weights (M−|m|)_+/M² on integers m. Their Fourier transform K_M is the squared absolute value of the normalized sum of M consecutive characters, so it is real, nonnegative, bounded by 1, and at t≠0 bounded by 1/(4M²||t||²). The last bound follows from |sin πt| ≥ 2||t||.

All frequencies in F with ||ξ·qθ|| ≤ η have phase one by the correction and contribute nonnegatively, including the constant term. The absolute total from other frequencies in F is at most 1/(4M²η²), and the total from outside F is less than 400^(−r)/100. The real part of the average is therefore positive. Some |m|<M gives a point of Q_(1/400) after the correction. Adding back u_i, the orbit at b=M+m satisfies

$$
0\le b\le 2M,\qquad \gamma+bq\theta\in z_i+Q_{1/200},\qquad i\ne0.
$$

This holds for every initial phase with the same θ. No union bound over starting positions is needed.

### A whole subprogression in the same box

Partition the torus into (400ℓ)^r half-open cubes of side 1/(400ℓ). Among the first (400ℓ)^r+1 multiples of qθ two fall in one cube. Their index difference h satisfies 1 ≤ h ≤ (400ℓ)^r and ||hqθ||_∞ ≤ 1/(400ℓ). Starting at b, the ℓ indices b+jh (0 ≤ j < ℓ) all lie below L. Their orbit points lie in z_i+Q_(1/100), since 1/200 + (ℓ−1)/(400ℓ) < 1/100. This proves the subprogression statement for every L-term progression, phase, and positive step admissible in [N].

### Patches and extensions

For a phase β let the physical cell of center i be the sites x ∈ [N] with β+xθ in z_i+Q_(1/100). If three occupied sites satisfy x+z=2y, their center second difference lies inside Q_(4/100), so all three center labels agree. The cells are disjoint as well, by applying this argument to three copies of a point with two potential labels.

Take P to be the cell for z_0. Take B to be the sites of H in all other cells. Every L-term progression contains an ℓ-term subprogression in one auxiliary cell by the preceding argument with γ=β+aθ, where a is its starting site. That subprogression has positive step hq and lies in [N], so it meets H there and hence meets B. For any k-term progression with k≥3 in P ∪ B, consecutive triples propagate a common cell label across all its terms. If its terms lie in T ∪ B for T ⊆ P, they must all lie in T or all in H. A k-free T and a 3-free H rule out both possibilities.

Finally take all shifts β+t/50, with t ranging over the r-dimensional grid of residues modulo 50. The distinguished boxes have side 1/50 and tile the torus up to their boundaries. Choose β outside the finitely many coordinate walls for the N tested sites. Their inverse images P then partition [N]. For each shift retain the associated B. These choices give the finite-parameter theorem.

### Consequences

Cao's Theorem 1.1 gives a 3-free H on [N] with ℓ ≤ exp(C₀√log N) for all large N. Indeed w(3,ℓ) ≥ exp(c(log ℓ)²) > N for an appropriate ℓ, and one restricts a two-color witness to [N]. Inserting this value gives

$$
\log L\le C_1\big(r\sqrt{\log N}+(\log N)/r\big).
$$

For fixed ε take r ≥ max(r₀,⌈4000/ε⌉) minimal. From the explicit formula, the term 2M+1 has exponent in N at most 1/s ≤ ε/4, up to a factor depending only on r. The term (ℓ−1)(400ℓ)^r is N^o(1) at fixed r. Thus L ≤ ⌈N^ε⌉ eventually, which proves the partition theorem. Any longer progression contains its initial L-term progression.

For **any** function w(N) tending to infinity, there is a length L(N)=N^o(1) for which at most w(N) parts suffice. Take r to be the smaller of ⌊log w(N)/log 50⌋ and ⌊(log N)^(1/4)⌋. Then r tends to infinity and r/√log N tends to zero. In particular an arbitrary prescribed k-free set can retain at least 1/w(N) of its weight while obtaining a blocker for all these progressions. Taking r of order (log N)^(1/4) gives the more explicit simultaneous scales exp(O((log N)^(1/4))) parts and L ≤ exp(O((log N)^(3/4))).

There is also an encoding of the entire family. Write I for all k-free sets and J for its L-hitting members. For a partition with m parts, the map S ↦ ((S ∩ P_i) ∪ B_i)_i takes I injectively into J^m. Intersecting coordinate i with P_i recovers the restriction of S to that part. Hence |I| ≤ |J|^m, and for each λ≥1 the partition functions satisfy ∑_(S∈I) λ^|S| ≤ (∑_(A∈J) λ^|A|)^m.

## Verification scope

[Four Lean theorems](RothRamseyGlue.lean) check propagation of labels, transfer of a blocker through an embedded subprogression, finite weighted averaging, and injectivity of the family encoding. The rotation, Fourier estimate, and asymptotic choice of parameters are written arguments with the external analytic inputs above.

[The prime-modulus construction in Lean](RothRamseyResidues.lean) proves a second version from first principles. If p is a prime ≥3, pℓ ≤ L, and N ≤ p(L−1), keep the prescribed sites in residue −t modulo p and the blocker sites in residue 1−t. Each admissible step is a unit modulo p. Every L-window has an ℓ-term subprogression in the auxiliary residue. Two distinct residue labels from {0,1} cannot form a modular midpoint triple, since their ordinary pair sums are less than p. For N=61, ℓ=10 and L=30, the kernel checks the blocker itself and the complete extension theorem for **all** k≥3 and all k-free inputs. The three 30-hitting extensions together cover the supplied set.

[An integer verifier](check_roth_ramsey_glue.py) builds two independent 3-free inputs on 4,007 sites and uses five residue shifts. The supplied set misses a progression of length 975. The verifier checks all 31,440 window/shift pairs, directly tests the resulting sets for forbidden triples, and reconstructs the weighted membership count across all five extensions. This finite example uses the same gluing implication and a one-dimensional modular realization of the cells.
