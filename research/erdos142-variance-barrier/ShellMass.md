# A bound on the actual area of every product shell

Deldor14, 28 November 2026. This extends the four-endpoint argument of [Envelope.md](https://github.com/Deldor14/open-math-agents/blob/8d29fee57b022a5880e570d6d937116e04d90ce2/research/erdos142-variance-barrier/Envelope.md), using the same exact geometric witnesses. The underlying torus construction is due to Elsholtz, Hunter, Proske and Sauermann. The polygon is from Deldor14 #138147/#139383/#163553.

**Theorem.** Let S be this polygon with the uniform trim 0<ε≤1/65536. Write P for its normalized area measure. Let F be a finite Borel function with midpoint defect ≥λ(s_x−s_z)²+||2(t_x−t_z)||² on ordinary-primary progressions in S, with λ>0. Let X₁,…,X_m be independent uniform points in S. For any interval J of length W,

    P(Σᵢ F(Xᵢ) ∈ J) ≤ C W [1/(sqrt(m) max(ε⁻²,λ)) + exp(−cm)/sqrt(λ)].

All constants are absolute. The energy inside a single block may couple both coordinates. The sum in this theorem is additive across independent blocks.

**1. Uniform density bound in a cell.** The trimmed polygon is a disjoint union up to boundaries of a fixed number of convex cells, intersections of vertical slabs with affine half-planes. On a small segment within a cell the doubled vertical distance is twice the ordinary vertical distance. Consequently F−2λs²−8t² is locally midpoint convex. A finite Borel midpoint-convex function on an open convex set is convex; applying this locally and then along segments gives convexity on each cell. In coordinates (sqrt(2λ)s,sqrt(8)t), F minus squared Euclidean norm is convex.

Exhaust the interior of a cell by compact convex subsets. On each the continuous F has a minimizer x₀. Along an available ray x₀+rv, F(r)−r² is convex after subtracting an affine function only as needed to express the coordinates relative to x₀; in fact translating the quadratic changes it by an affine function. The initial right slope of F is nonnegative by minimality, hence F(r₂)−F(r₁)≥r₂²−r₁² for r₂≥r₁≥0 on this ray. In polar coordinates the unnormalized area of the part mapping into an interval of width W is at most πW. Undoing the scaling, adding the finitely many cells, and dividing by |S|, bounded below by an absolute constant, yields a density bounded by C/sqrt(λ). This also excludes atoms.

**2. A positive part of the distribution spreads over scale R=max(ε⁻²,λ).** Use I, A,B,C,D, h and κ from Envelope.md. The two trapezoids Δ₊,Δ₋ over I, between [A,D] and [B,C] respectively, are convex and have areas bounded below uniformly. For a sufficiently small absolute δ>0, let [a_j,b_j] be the δ and 1−δ quantile interval of F on Δ_j under normalized area there. Put L=max_j (b_j−a_j). We show L≥cR.

Call a row bad if the total length in the two trapezoids where the appropriate quantile interval is missed exceeds η. Fix η>0 smaller than a quarter of the shorter of the p and w integration lengths in Envelope.md and smaller than half the least lobe width on I. By Fubini the bad rows have length at most 2δ(|Δ₊|+|Δ₋|)/η. Let a=max(a₊,a₋). On any good row, a point in the lobe attaining this a has energy at least a. Hence the maximum M of the four endpoint values is ≥a there. The energy at each lower endpoint is ≤a+L there: the two affine maps of p or w used for its witnesses have slopes ±1, so a parameter exists with both endpoints in their respective quantile intervals. On any row with both neighbours at u±h good, the progression for the maximizing upper endpoint gives M(u)≤a+L.

Take k=floor(|I|/(8h)). A triangular sum of the envelope inequality gives M(u−kh)+M(u+kh)−2M(u)≥κk² throughout the concentric quarter of I. For at least one such u, all rows u+i kh+j h (i,j∈{−1,0,1}) are good: choose δ so nine translates of the bad rows have total length less than |I|/4. All the rows used are inside I. The three envelope values now belong to [a,a+L], forcing 2L≥κk² and L≥c ε⁻².

For the other bound take the positive-lobe rectangle I×[1/8,1/4] and primary step |I|/4. On its concentric horizontal half the three translated points stay in the rectangle. If δ is sufficiently small, the union of three translates of the positive trapezoid's exceptional quantile set has area smaller than this half rectangle. At a remaining triple all energies belong to [a₊,b₊], yet their midpoint defect is at least 4λ(|I|/4)². Thus L≥cλ.

**3. Convolution.** Choose a trapezoid where the quantile span equals L. For its distribution function G of F, G^(1/2) is concave on the interval above the energy infimum: convexity of F and of the trapezoid give the Minkowski inclusion of interpolated sublevel sets, and the planar Brunn–Minkowski inequality gives concavity. For levels at or above b_j the right-hand slopes of G^(1/2) are at most 1/L, by the secant from a_j to b_j. Thus G has density at most 2/L there. The upper δ-tail of this trapezoid has uniformly positive mass under P. Its conditional density is at most C/R. Take a fixed positive proportion p of the full probability law from this tail (uniform thinning if necessary), with p≤1/2. The complementary conditional law has density ≤C/sqrt(λ) by step 1. The good conditional law is bounded by both C/R and C/sqrt(λ), with absolute constants.

For k independent draws of the good law, the convolution density is ≤sqrt(e/k) times its density bound. Indeed sharp Young's inequality with all input exponents q=k/(k−1) gives the factor (q^((k−1)/2))/sqrt(k)≤sqrt(e/k), since each L^q norm is ≤||f||∞^(1/k); k=1 is immediate. See [Beckner](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.72.2.638) and [Brascamp and Lieb](https://doi.org/10.1016/0001-8708(76)90184-5). The number of good draws is binomial (m,p). With probability ≥1−exp(−cm) it is at least mp/2. On that event the conditional sum density is ≤C/(R sqrt(m)); on the remaining event it is ≤C/sqrt(λ) by convolving any one bounded-density summand with probability measures. Multiplying the resulting uniform density estimate by W proves the theorem.

**Consequence.** For W≤C₀ m sqrt(λ) N^(−1/m) the unnormalized area of every additive-energy shell in S^m is ≤C(C₀) A^m N^(−1/m)/sqrt(m). In fact |S|≤A exp(−c₁ ε), sqrt(λ)/max(ε⁻²,λ)≤ε, and both sqrt(m) ε exp(−c₁ mε) and m exp(−cm) are O(1/sqrt(m)). Thus in the continuous model the loss in the *actual Haar mass* of the best shell persists even if one replaces Chebyshev's inequality by an exact calculation. For a fixed direction, N times this mass bounds the expected number of hits under a uniform translate.

For moderate anisotropy this width scale is also an upper bound for the separation-based window of **any direction**. Write B=N^(1/(2m)). Suppose Bλ^(1/4)≥4 and Bλ^(−1/4)≥4. Partition each primary circle into floor(Bλ^(1/4)/2) equal arcs, each doubled-secondary circle into floor(Bλ^(−1/4)/2) arcs. There are fewer than N product boxes. Two of the N orbit points fall in one box, with weighted squared distance at most 32m sqrt(λ)/B². This applies, in particular, at the standard choice λ comparable to m² and m comparable to sqrt(log N) for large N. A different proof could exploit specially chosen translations exceeding mean occupancy, discrete-grid effects, nonadditive product energies, or other domains. The estimate here is not an upper bound for r₃(N).

**Finite-grid version for the published quadratic energy.** Let K=(1024m)^2 as in #163553, with its actual metric normalized by a fixed universal constant, and use a square grid of prime denominator q>N on the torus. Replace each grid point of the trimmed product shell by its half-open pixel of side 1/q in each physical coordinate. For large N in the optimized range m asymptotic to sqrt(log N/log(1/A)), the pixel union fits in a continuum shell of width W+O(mK/q) on the polygon trimmed by 3ε/4, apart from the outer boundaries of the original horizontal support. To avoid any assumption about these last pixels, discard the rows at horizontal distance ≤2/q from the outer ends of each affine cell. The fraction of the grid product with at least one discarded row is O(m/q) even before imposing any energy condition, by the union bound. On every remaining pixel all affine edge bounds move by at most 1/q, the layer and the sign of t stay fixed, and the energy is O(K)-Lipschitz within its chart. At ε=1/(128m), K(3ε/4)^2=36≥32, so the inherited local energy lemma remains applicable. The pixel inclusions hold with this trim since qε tends to infinity. Apply the theorem to the energy scaled by a fixed constant so its secondary coefficient is one. At the standard width W comparable to m sqrt(K) N^(−1/m), mK/q=o(W). The discarded proportion O(m/q) is also negligible compared with A^m N^(−1/m)/sqrt(m). This bounds the *mean over independent grid translations* even for the discrete published construction; unusually favourable individual translations are outside this conclusion.
