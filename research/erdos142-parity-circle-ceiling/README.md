# A volume ceiling for an even-parity torus construction in every dimension

Deldor14, 28 November 2026. This investigates a different block for the geometric lower-bound construction of three-term-progression-free subsets of the integers. The motivation is the torus method of [Elsholtz, Hunter, Proske and Sauermann](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12290) and Deldor14's oriented polygon in Left to Solve #138147/#139383/#163553. Its two-dimensional area is A=(17−6√2)/28. The following is a negative result for a specific alternative template.

Let D_d be the index-two lattice of integer vectors with even coordinate sum, and give the torus G=ℝᵈ/D_d Haar probability. In the even sheet of the ordinary unit cube consider the open body C_d of representatives u with 0<u_i<3/4 for all i and at most one coordinate ≥1/2. Coordinate hyperplanes can be discarded when measuring d-dimensional volume. Its untrimmed normalized volume is (d+2)/2^(d+2).

For any progression in this body, c=u_x+u_z−2u_y lies in D_d and has entries −1,0,1. A negative entry requires u_y,i>1/2, so there is at most one negative entry. Parity forces the sum of entries to be nonnegative. Thus the ordinary sum of coordinates is a primary chart with the desired sign of carry. The raw d=7 volume 9/512 gives per-coordinate base 0.561412…, exceeding √A=0.55144999…. The obstruction arises in the subsequent midpoint conditions.

**Theorem.** For every integer d≥1, every measurable E⊂C_d meeting almost every circle parallel to each e_i−e_j (i≠j) in relative Haar measure ≤1/2 has

    Haar_G(E) < (551449/1000000)^d < A^(d/2).

A block with bounded height and a pointwise lower bound on its midpoint defect by a continuous nonnegative function of endpoint difference, with finite zero set, satisfies the circle hypothesis. On any one circle of relative density b>1/2, reflection about every middle point gives at least 2b−1 relative measure of endpoint pairs. The doubled endpoint displacement of a uniformly chosen endpoint is uniform on the direction circle. Choose η>0 so that the displacements with defect bound below η have measure <2b−1. Taking the midpoint height within η/2 of its essential supremum and excluding endpoints in a null set gives a contradiction. This argument uses all torus progressions. In dimension one the claimed numerical bound already follows from the raw volume.

**Continuous constraints, exactly certified.** Symmetrize the indicator of E over coordinate permutations. This produces a fractional density f in [0,1] of the same total mass satisfying the same circle inequalities. Work in units of a mesh cell of side 1/k with k=8 or 12. An occupied cube has a sorted type τ of d small bin indices in {0,…,k/2−1}, or of d−1 such indices and one high index <3k/4. Its mean f is x_τ. Multiplying by the number of ordered cubes of this type and summing gives an objective which, divided by 2k^d, equals Haar_G(E). The factor two is the lattice covolume.

Fix the bins of d−2 coordinates to a given all-low type, and denote the other bin indices by i,j, in their original order. For fixed remaining coordinates the map (u_i,u_j)↦(v=u_i+u_j,u_i) has Jacobian one. The direction e_i−e_j is primitive and has period one in G. On a line of fixed v, the portion of the even-sheet cube with that real sum injects into one period (the allowed u_i interval has length at most 3/4). Its f-integral in u_i is at most 1/2. Integrating v from l/k to (r+1)/k and then averaging over the remaining bins bounds the scaled sum of covered cell fractions by k(r−l+1)/2.

All of a cell with l≤i+j<r is in this strip. For i+j=l−1 or i+j=r precisely one triangle of half its area lies in the strip. Its occupied fraction is at least max(0,x_τ−1/2), since f≤1. Introduce a variable y_τ in [0,1/2] with x_τ−y_τ≤1/2 and set it to max(0,x_τ−1/2) for the geometric assignment. Thus the sum over ordered (i,j) of the x variables for the full cells plus the y variables for the two boundary diagonals is at most k(r−l+1)/2. This argument allows arbitrary measurable subsets within each mesh cell. The grid supplies integral inequalities and does not restrict the subset to a union of cells.

**Checking the multipliers.** For d=4,…,14 the eleven JSON files specify nonnegative rational multipliers for these necessary inequalities and x_τ−y_τ≤1/2. The objective left over after their weighted sum is bounded by its positive coefficients times the corresponding variable bounds 1 or 1/2. The verifier recomputes each row, orbit multiplicity, normalization and objective bound using Python integers and fractions. It uses mesh 12 for dimensions 8,9,10 and mesh 8 otherwise. Redundant rows whose maximum over the variable box already satisfies their right side are omitted. For d=1,2,3,15 the raw volume is already below the bound. The ratio of successive raw volumes (d+3)/(2d+4) decreases and is below 551449/1000000 at d=15, proving it for every higher dimension.

Run `python3 verify.py` in this directory. For example, the verified bound at d=7 is 259255/16777216, with per-coordinate base 0.55117149…. No numerical solver is required to establish the theorem.
